part 3: the first farmers

when the ice age ended, people in several parts of the world, each on their own, began planting crops and raising animals. this change, called the agricultural revolution or the neolithic revolution, let people settle in one place, grow in number, and build villages. it is one of the biggest turning points in all of history.

the world warms up

as the ice age ended, the great ice sheets melted, sea levels rose, and the climate became warmer and more stable. land bridges like beringia disappeared under the sea. forests and grasslands spread, and wild grains grew in big patches in some regions.

people who gathered these wild grains began to stay in one place for longer. over many generations, they started saving the best seeds and planting them on purpose. this was the start of farming.

the fertile crescent

the earliest known farming began in the fertile crescent, a curved strip of good land in the middle east that runs from the persian gulf through modern iraq, syria, and turkey, down to the eastern mediterranean coast.

farmers there grew wheat, barley, lentils, peas, and flax. they tamed sheep, goats, pigs, and cattle. famous early sites include jericho, one of the oldest towns in the world, and göbekli tepe in turkey, a huge ring of carved stone pillars built by people who were only just beginning to settle down.

farming everywhere else

farming was not invented just once. people in different parts of the world figured it out on their own, using the plants that grew near them:

  • china: rice along the yangtze river and millet along the yellow river. pigs and chickens were tamed.
  • new guinea: taro, bananas, and sugarcane.
  • africa: sorghum, yams, and african rice. cattle herding spread across the sahara, which was green and wet at the time.
  • mexico: maize (corn), beans, and squash, known as the "three sisters".
  • the andes: potatoes and quinoa. llamas and alpacas were tamed.
  • eastern north america: sunflowers and squash.

from these starting points, farming slowly spread outward, reaching most of the world's good land over time.

taming animals

people also tamed, or domesticated, animals. the dog had already been tamed back in the ice age. now came sheep, goats, cattle, pigs, chickens, and later donkeys, horses, and camels.

animals gave meat, milk, wool, leather, and manure for the fields. later, they were used to pull plows and carts and to carry people and goods. horses in particular would change warfare and travel across the whole of eurasia.

the first villages

because farmers had to stay close to their fields, they built permanent homes. villages grew up, some with hundreds or even thousands of people. çatalhöyük in turkey was a big town of mud-brick houses packed so tightly together that people walked across the rooftops and entered their homes through holes in the roof.

settled life led to new inventions: pottery for storing food, weaving for cloth, polished stone tools, and better ways of building. people also started to own things, like land, animals, and stored grain.

good news and bad news

farming could feed far more people from the same patch of land than hunting and gathering, so populations grew fast. but farming also brought problems:

  • farmers often worked longer hours than hunter-gatherers.
  • diets became less varied, and people were often shorter and less healthy.
  • living close to animals and to each other spread new diseases.
  • a bad harvest could mean famine.
  • stored food and land were worth fighting over, and differences between rich and poor began to grow.

still, there was no going back. once the population grew, only farming could feed everyone.

stone to metal

at first, all tools were made of stone, wood, and bone. then people learned to work metal. copper came first. later, people learned to mix copper with tin to make bronze, which was much harder. this began the bronze age.

other important inventions arrived around this time, including the wheel, the plow, and the sailboat. these would help the first cities and kingdoms grow, which is the story of part 4.
