part 5: the classical world

in the classical age, huge empires ruled millions of people. persia, greece, rome, india, and china produced philosophers, scientists, and religious teachers whose ideas still shape the world. trade routes like the silk roads linked east and west for the first time.

an age of big ideas

at around the same time, in different parts of the world, great thinkers asked deep questions about how to live, what is right, and what the world is made of. some historians call this the "axial age".

  • china: confucius taught respect for family, good manners, and wise government. laozi, founder of daoism, taught living in harmony with nature, called the dao.
  • india: siddhartha gautama, the buddha, taught that suffering comes from craving and can be ended by following the eightfold path. mahavira taught jainism and non-violence. the upanishads developed hindu ideas about the soul.
  • persia: zoroaster taught that the world is a battle between good and evil.
  • israel: the hebrew prophets taught about one god and justice for the poor. the hebrew bible took shape.
  • greece: philosophers like socrates, plato, and aristotle used reason to ask about truth, justice, and nature.

the persian empire

cyrus the great founded the achaemenid persian empire, which became the largest empire the world had yet seen, stretching from egypt and turkey to the edge of india. cyrus let conquered peoples keep their own customs and religions, and he allowed the jews exiled in babylon to return home.

darius the great organized the empire into provinces, built the royal road with messengers on horseback, and used standard coins. the persians would later go to war with the greeks.

ancient greece

greece was not one country but many independent city-states, each called a polis. the two most famous were athens and sparta. athens invented democracy, where male citizens voted on laws themselves. sparta was a military state where boys were trained from childhood to be soldiers.

the greek city-states joined together to beat back two persian invasions, with famous battles at marathon, thermopylae, and salamis. afterwards, athens had a golden age. it built the parthenon, and its writers created drama (both tragedy and comedy) and history writing. later, athens and sparta fought each other in the long peloponnesian war, which weakened them both.

the greeks also made huge advances in math, science, and medicine. pythagoras and euclid worked on geometry, hippocrates is called the father of medicine, and archimedes made discoveries in physics. the olympic games were held in honor of zeus.

alexander the great

philip of macedon conquered the greek city-states. his son, alexander, who had been taught by aristotle, then led his army on an incredible campaign. he conquered the whole persian empire, egypt, and reached all the way into india, never losing a battle.

alexander died young, and his empire was split among his generals. but greek language and culture spread across the middle east. this blended greek-and-eastern culture is called hellenistic. its greatest city was alexandria in egypt, home of a famous library and a giant lighthouse.

rome

rome began as a small town in italy. it threw out its kings and became a republic, ruled by elected officials and a senate. the roman army was highly disciplined, and rome slowly conquered all of italy. it then fought three wars against carthage, a trading power in north africa. in one, the carthaginian general hannibal crossed the alps with elephants, but rome won in the end and destroyed carthage.

as rome grew, fights between rich and poor and between powerful generals tore the republic apart. julius caesar took control and was murdered by senators. his adopted son, octavian, won the civil wars that followed, took the name augustus, and became the first roman emperor.

the roman empire surrounded the whole mediterranean sea. there followed a long period of peace called the pax romana. the romans built roads, aqueducts, bridges, the colosseum, and cities across europe, north africa, and the middle east. latin, the language of rome, is the ancestor of italian, french, spanish, portuguese, and romanian, and roman law shaped legal systems long after rome was gone.

in the roman province of judea, a jewish teacher named jesus of nazareth preached love, forgiveness, and the kingdom of god. he was crucified by the romans. his followers believed he rose from the dead, and christianity spread across the empire, despite persecution. the emperor constantine later became a christian, and eventually it became the official religion of the empire.

india

after alexander left india, chandragupta maurya united most of the subcontinent into the maurya empire. his grandson, ashoka, fought a terrible war to conquer the kingdom of kalinga. horrified by the bloodshed, he turned to buddhism, gave up conquest, and carved messages about kindness and non-violence onto pillars and rocks across his empire. he sent buddhist missionaries abroad, helping buddhism spread to sri lanka and beyond.

later, the gupta empire brought a golden age. indian mathematicians developed the decimal number system and the concept of zero. these "arabic numerals" are the numbers the whole world uses today. great works of hindu literature, like the mahabharata and the ramayana, took their lasting form.

china unites

after the zhou dynasty weakened, china split into rival states that fought each other for a long time, called the warring states period. the state of qin won, and its king became qin shi huang, the first emperor of all china. he standardized writing, money, weights, and even the width of cart axles. he began joining walls together into an early great wall, and he was buried with an army of thousands of life-sized clay soldiers, the terracotta army.

the qin was harsh and did not last long. the han dynasty came next and ruled for a very long time. it made confucianism the official philosophy, chose officials partly through exams, and invented paper. the han were so important that the largest ethnic group in china still calls itself "han chinese".

the silk roads

the silk roads were a network of trade routes linking china, central asia, india, persia, and the mediterranean. chinese silk traveled west to rome. horses, glass, wool, and silver traveled east. sea routes across the indian ocean linked africa, arabia, india, and southeast asia.

ideas and religions also traveled these roads. buddhism spread from india into central asia and china. sadly, diseases traveled too.

the rest of the world

  • africa: the kingdom of kush continued in the city of meroë, famous for ironworking and its own pyramids. later, the kingdom of aksum in ethiopia became a great trading power and one of the first states to adopt christianity. bantu-speaking peoples spread farming and ironworking across central and southern africa.
  • the americas: the maya built city-states with stepped pyramids in the jungles of central america, developed a full writing system, advanced math with a zero, and a complex calendar. the huge city of teotihuacan rose in the valley of mexico. in peru, the nazca drew giant lines and animal shapes in the desert, and the moche made fine pottery.
  • the pacific: polynesian sailors, using only the stars, winds, and waves to navigate, began settling the islands of the pacific ocean.
