after the second world war, the united states and the soviet union faced off in a long cold war under the shadow of nuclear weapons. the old empires fell apart and dozens of new nations were born. people around the world fought for equal rights. humans walked on the moon, and computers and the internet connected the whole planet. this is the world we live in now.

a new world order

to prevent another world war, countries set up the united nations (un). it wrote the universal declaration of human rights, which says that all people everywhere are born free and equal in dignity and rights.

the united states helped rebuild western europe with money through the marshall plan. germany and japan became peaceful democracies. european countries began working together more closely, which grew into the european union. after the holocaust, the state of israel was founded, which led to wars with its arab neighbors and a long, still unresolved conflict with the palestinians.

the cold war

the united states (capitalist and democratic) and the soviet union (communist and a one-party state) became rival superpowers. they never fought each other directly, which is why it is called the cold war. instead, they competed through an arms race, spying, propaganda, and wars fought by other countries.

europe was divided by an "iron curtain". the west formed the military alliance nato, and the soviets formed the warsaw pact. germany was split into east and west, and the berlin wall was built across the city of berlin to stop east germans from escaping to the west.

both sides built thousands of nuclear weapons, enough to destroy the world many times over. the closest they came to nuclear war was the cuban missile crisis, when the soviets placed missiles in cuba, just off the coast of the united states. after tense days, both sides backed down.

the cold war turned hot in other places. the korean war left korea split into a communist north and a capitalist south. in the vietnam war, the united states fought to stop communist north vietnam from taking over the south, but failed after years of fighting. the soviets fought a long, failed war in afghanistan.

communist china

after a long civil war, the communists led by mao zedong won control of china and founded the people's republic of china. the losing nationalists fled to taiwan. mao's "great leap forward", an attempt to industrialize quickly, caused a famine that killed tens of millions. his "cultural revolution" brought years of chaos and persecution.

after mao died, deng xiaoping opened china's economy to the market while keeping the communist party in charge. china grew at an amazing speed, lifting hundreds of millions out of poverty and becoming the world's second largest economy. however, the government still crushes dissent, as it did in the tiananmen square protests.

the end of empires

weakened by the war and facing independence movements everywhere, the european empires broke apart. this is called decolonization.

  • india won independence from britain after gandhi's long non-violent campaign. it was split into india and pakistan, and the partition led to mass violence and one of the largest migrations in history. later, bangladesh broke away from pakistan.
  • indonesia won independence from the netherlands, and vietnam from france.
  • across africa, dozens of countries gained independence. ghana, led by kwame nkrumah, was the first in sub-saharan africa. algeria won independence from france after a brutal war. kenya's independence followed the mau mau uprising.
  • many new nations faced hard challenges, including borders drawn by colonizers, poverty, civil wars, and cold war interference.

in south africa, a white minority government enforced apartheid, a system of strict racial segregation. nelson mandela, a leader of the struggle against it, spent twenty-seven years in prison. apartheid finally ended, and mandela became south africa's first black president.

the fight for equal rights

in the united states, the civil rights movement fought to end segregation and racial discrimination. rosa parks refused to give up her bus seat, sparking a boycott. martin luther king jr. led non-violent marches and gave his famous "i have a dream" speech. new laws banned segregation and protected voting rights. king was later assassinated.

other movements fought for rights too: the women's movement pushed for equal pay and equal treatment, and the lgbtq rights movement fought against discrimination and, in many countries, won the right to same-sex marriage. indigenous peoples, disabled people, and many others organized for their rights around the world.

the space race

the cold war rivals competed in space. the soviet union launched sputnik, the first artificial satellite, and sent the first human into space, yuri gagarin. the united states answered with the apollo program.

on the apollo 11 mission, neil armstrong and buzz aldrin became the first people to walk on the moon. armstrong's words were "that's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind." later came space stations, the space shuttle, the international space station, telescopes like hubble, and robot rovers on mars.

the end of the cold war

the soviet economy was struggling. its new leader, mikhail gorbachev, tried to reform it with more openness and restructuring. but change swept faster than he expected. across eastern europe, people rose up peacefully against communist rule, and in berlin, crowds tore down the berlin wall. germany was reunited.

soon after, the soviet union itself collapsed, breaking into fifteen separate countries, including russia and ukraine. the cold war was over, and the united states was left as the world's only superpower.

a global world

the world became more connected than ever through globalization: goods, money, people, and ideas flowing across borders. container ships, cheap flights, and multinational companies tied economies together. countries like china, india, south korea, and brazil grew rapidly.

but there were also problems. the gap between rich and poor grew in many places. al-qaeda terrorists attacked new york and washington, leading to long wars in afghanistan and iraq. a global financial crisis hit the world economy hard. civil wars in places like syria forced millions from their homes, and russia launched a full-scale invasion of ukraine.

computers and the internet

the first electronic computers filled whole rooms. the invention of the transistor and the microchip made them smaller, cheaper, and far more powerful. personal computers arrived in homes and offices.

the internet grew out of a network built for the us military and universities. tim berners-lee invented the world wide web, which made the internet easy to use for everyone, and websites like this one were born. then came search engines, email, online shopping, social media, and smartphones, which put the whole internet in people's pockets. most recently, artificial intelligence has become a major new technology.

the world today

there are now around eight billion people on earth. people live longer, healthier lives than ever before, thanks to vaccines, antibiotics, and better food and sanitation. far fewer people live in extreme poverty than in the past, and more people can read than ever.

but big challenges remain. climate change, caused mostly by burning coal, oil, and gas, is warming the planet, melting ice, raising sea levels, and making weather more extreme. a worldwide covid pandemic shut down much of the world and killed millions. war, inequality, and threats to democracy continue.

history is not over. it is still being written, by everyone alive today.
