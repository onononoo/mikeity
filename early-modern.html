for the first time, the whole world became connected. european ships crossed the oceans and reached the americas, africa, and asia. this brought new trade, new foods, and new ideas, but also conquest, deadly disease, and the enslavement of millions of africans. at the same time, the printing press, the reformation, and the scientific revolution changed how people thought.

the renaissance

renaissance means "rebirth". it began in the rich trading cities of italy, like florence and venice. scholars rediscovered the books of ancient greece and rome, many of them brought by greek scholars fleeing the ottomans. a new way of thinking, called humanism, focused on human abilities and life in this world, not just the afterlife.

artists studied the human body and used perspective to make paintings look real. leonardo da vinci painted the mona lisa and filled notebooks with inventions. michelangelo carved the statue of david and painted the ceiling of the sistine chapel. rich families like the medici paid for art and learning. the renaissance then spread across the rest of europe.

the printing press

in germany, johannes gutenberg built a printing press using metal movable type. before this, books in europe were copied by hand and were rare and expensive. now they could be made quickly and cheaply. the first big book he printed was the bible.

printing spread across europe very fast. more people learned to read, and new ideas, whether about religion, science, or politics, could reach thousands of people. many historians count it as one of the most important inventions ever.

the gunpowder empires

using cannons and guns, three great muslim empires rose across the middle east and south asia:

  • the ottoman empire: the ottoman turks captured constantinople, ending the byzantine empire, and renamed it istanbul. under suleiman the magnificent, the ottomans ruled the balkans, the middle east, and north africa, and even besieged vienna.
  • the safavid empire: in persia (iran), the safavids made shia islam the state religion, which it still is today. their capital, isfahan, was famous for its beauty.
  • the mughal empire: in india, the mughals ruled most of the subcontinent. akbar was known for tolerating all religions. his grandson shah jahan built the taj mahal as a tomb for his wife.

in russia, the princes of moscow threw off mongol rule, and ivan the terrible became the first tsar. russia began expanding east across siberia.

voyages of discovery

europeans wanted to reach the spices and silks of asia without going through ottoman and italian middlemen. portugal led the way. prince henry the navigator sponsored voyages down the coast of africa. bartolomeu dias sailed around the southern tip of africa, and vasco da gama reached india by sea.

sailing for spain, christopher columbus tried to reach asia by sailing west across the atlantic. instead he reached the caribbean, opening the americas to europe. later, ferdinand magellan's expedition became the first to sail all the way around the world, although magellan himself was killed on the way.

earlier, china had sent huge treasure fleets under the admiral zheng he as far as east africa, with ships far bigger than columbus's. but china's rulers then stopped the voyages and turned inward.

conquest of the americas

spanish soldiers, called conquistadors, came to the americas looking for gold. hernán cortés, with the help of native peoples who hated aztec rule, conquered the aztec empire. francisco pizarro conquered the inca empire, which had been weakened by a civil war.

the biggest killer, though, was disease. native americans had no immunity to european diseases like smallpox, measles, and flu. these diseases killed a huge share of the native population, perhaps as many as nine out of every ten people in some areas. spain and portugal built colonial empires across central and south america. later, england, france, and the netherlands set up colonies in north america and the caribbean.

the columbian exchange

the meeting of the old world and the new led to a huge swap of plants, animals, and diseases, called the columbian exchange.

from the americas to the worldfrom the old world to the americas
maize (corn), potatoes, tomatoes, chili peppers, cacao (chocolate), tobacco, turkeys, peanutshorses, cattle, pigs, sheep, wheat, sugarcane, coffee, bananas, and diseases like smallpox

potatoes and maize fed fast-growing populations in europe, africa, and china. horses changed life for native peoples of the great plains. silver from mines in peru and mexico flowed around the world, much of it to china.

the atlantic slave trade

european colonists set up huge plantations in the americas to grow sugar, and later tobacco, cotton, and coffee. with native populations devastated, they turned to enslaved africans for labor.

in the atlantic slave trade, more than twelve million africans were captured, packed into ships, and carried across the ocean in horrific conditions, a journey called the middle passage. many died on the way. those who survived were forced to work without pay, were bought and sold as property, and their children were born into slavery. it was one of the greatest crimes in history, and its effects are still felt today.

the reformation

many people felt the catholic church had become corrupt. in germany, a monk named martin luther wrote his 95 theses attacking the church's sale of "indulgences", which promised to reduce punishment for sins. he taught that people are saved by faith alone and that the bible, not the pope, is the highest authority. thanks to the printing press, his ideas spread fast.

this split western christianity. the new churches were called protestant. john calvin started another branch in switzerland. in england, king henry viii broke from the pope so he could end his marriage, and made himself head of the church of england.

the catholic church answered with the counter-reformation, reforming itself and founding new orders like the jesuits. but catholics and protestants fought many bitter religious wars across europe.

east asia

in china, the ming dynasty drove out the mongols, rebuilt the great wall into the form we know today, and built the forbidden city in beijing. the ming was later replaced by the qing dynasty, founded by the manchus from the north, which became the last dynasty of china.

in japan, after a long period of civil war between rival lords, the country was united and the tokugawa shogunate took power. it brought a long peace but closed japan off from most of the outside world, allowing only very limited trade with the dutch and chinese.

the scientific revolution

scientists began to test ideas through observation and experiment, instead of just trusting ancient authorities. nicolaus copernicus said the earth goes around the sun, not the other way around. galileo galilei used a telescope to back this up, and was put on trial by the church for it. johannes kepler worked out how planets move in ovals.

finally, isaac newton explained gravity and the laws of motion, showing that the same rules work on earth and in space. others made breakthroughs in medicine, chemistry, and biology, and invented the microscope. the idea that nature follows laws that people can discover led straight into the enlightenment of part 8.
