part 8: the age of revolutions

thinkers of the enlightenment argued that people have natural rights and that governments should serve the people, not kings. these ideas sparked revolutions in america, france, haiti, and across latin america. at the same time, in britain, the industrial revolution began to change how people worked and lived.

the enlightenment

inspired by the scientific revolution, enlightenment thinkers believed that reason could be used to improve society, not just to understand nature. they questioned the power of kings and the church.

  • john locke said all people have natural rights to life, liberty, and property, and that if a government breaks those rights, people can overthrow it.
  • montesquieu said power should be split between different branches of government so no one gets too much.
  • voltaire argued for freedom of speech and freedom of religion.
  • jean-jacques rousseau said government should be based on the will of the people.
  • adam smith argued that free markets, driven by self-interest and competition, create wealth.
  • mary wollstonecraft argued that women deserve the same education and rights as men.

these ideas spread through books, newspapers, and coffeehouses, and inspired people to demand change.

the american revolution

britain had thirteen colonies along the east coast of north america. after an expensive war with france, britain taxed the colonists, who had no one representing them in the british parliament. they protested with the slogan "no taxation without representation", and at the boston tea party they dumped british tea into the harbor.

war broke out. the colonies signed the declaration of independence, written mostly by thomas jefferson, which said "all men are created equal". led by george washington and helped by france, the colonists won. they formed the united states of america and wrote a constitution with a president, a congress, and courts, plus a bill of rights.

but the new country's freedoms did not apply to everyone. slavery remained legal, women could not vote, and native americans would be pushed off their lands.

the french revolution

france was deep in debt, bread was expensive, and ordinary people paid most of the taxes while nobles and clergy paid little. king louis xvi called a meeting of representatives, and the common people's representatives declared themselves a national assembly. a crowd in paris stormed the bastille, a royal prison and symbol of the king's power.

the revolutionaries ended the privileges of the nobles and issued the declaration of the rights of man and of the citizen. their slogan was "liberty, equality, fraternity". the monarchy was abolished, and louis xvi and queen marie antoinette were executed by guillotine.

the revolution turned violent. during the reign of terror, led by maximilien robespierre, thousands of suspected enemies were executed. eventually robespierre himself was executed, and france was ruled by a weak government, which opened the way for a general to take over.

napoleon

napoleon bonaparte was a brilliant young general who seized power and later crowned himself emperor. his armies conquered most of europe. he spread some revolutionary ideas as he went, including the napoleonic code, a set of laws that made people equal before the law and is still the base of law in many countries.

napoleon's invasion of russia was a disaster. the russians retreated and burned their own land, and the freezing winter destroyed his army. he was defeated, exiled, escaped, and returned, but was finally beaten at the battle of waterloo and exiled for good. the great powers met at the congress of vienna to redraw the map of europe and bring back the old kings.

the haitian revolution

saint-domingue, a french colony in the caribbean, was the richest colony in the world, producing huge amounts of sugar and coffee through the brutal labor of enslaved africans. inspired partly by the french revolution, the enslaved people rose up.

led by toussaint louverture, a formerly enslaved man and brilliant leader, they defeated the french, spanish, and british. napoleon tried to retake the colony and restore slavery, but failed. the colony became haiti, the first independent nation in latin america and the caribbean, and the only country ever founded by a successful revolt of enslaved people.

latin american independence

when napoleon invaded spain and portugal, their american colonies saw a chance for freedom. simón bolívar, called "the liberator", led the fight for independence across northern south america, including venezuela, colombia, ecuador, peru, and bolivia (named after him). josé de san martín freed argentina and chile.

in mexico, a priest named miguel hidalgo called for revolt, and mexico eventually won independence. brazil broke from portugal peacefully, becoming an empire under a member of the portuguese royal family. soon, almost all of latin america was independent, though power mostly stayed in the hands of wealthy elites.

the industrial revolution begins

at the same time, a different kind of revolution began in britain. britain had lots of coal and iron, money to invest, colonies for trade, and a growing population. inventors made machines that could spin and weave cloth far faster than by hand.

james watt greatly improved the steam engine, which could power machines anywhere, not just next to rivers. factories sprang up, and people moved from the countryside to crowded cities to work in them. the first railways were built, and steam locomotives began carrying goods and people at speeds never seen before. this revolution continues in part 9.

the end of slavery

a movement to end slavery, called abolitionism, grew stronger. enslaved people resisted and rebelled, and campaigners like olaudah equiano, a formerly enslaved writer, and william wilberforce in britain pushed for change. britain banned the slave trade and later ended slavery in its empire.

other countries slowly followed. in the united states, the fight over slavery would lead to a civil war, which is covered in part 9.
