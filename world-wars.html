part 10: the world wars

rivalries between the great powers exploded into the first world war, a new kind of industrial war that killed millions. the peace that followed was shaky. revolution in russia, a worldwide economic crash, and the rise of dictators led to the second world war, the deadliest conflict in history, which included the holocaust and ended with the atomic bomb.

the road to war

historians often sum up the causes of the first world war with four words:

  • militarism: countries built up huge armies and navies. britain and germany raced to build the most battleships.
  • alliances: europe was split into two armed camps. the triple entente (france, russia, britain) faced the triple alliance (germany, austria-hungary, italy).
  • imperialism: the powers competed for colonies and influence.
  • nationalism: peoples like the serbs wanted their own nations, and great powers were fiercely proud.

the spark came in sarajevo, when archduke franz ferdinand, heir to the throne of austria-hungary, was assassinated by a young bosnian serb. austria-hungary declared war on serbia, and because of the alliances, within weeks nearly all of europe was at war.

the first world war

everyone expected a short war. instead, on the western front in france and belgium, both sides dug long lines of trenches and got stuck. soldiers lived in mud, among rats and disease, and died by the thousands trying to cross "no man's land" into machine gun fire. battles like the somme and verdun killed hundreds of thousands for tiny gains.

new weapons included poison gas, tanks, airplanes, and submarines. the war was fought around the world, including in the middle east, africa, and at sea. soldiers from the colonies, like india, australia, and africa, fought too. in the ottoman empire, the government carried out the armenian genocide, killing around a million armenians.

after german submarines sank ships carrying americans, the united states joined the war against germany. with its armies exhausted, its people starving, and revolution breaking out at home, germany agreed to an armistice. around twenty million people, soldiers and civilians, had died. soon after, a flu pandemic killed even more people around the world.

the russian revolution

russia was badly beaten in the war, and its people were starving. the tsar, nicholas ii, was forced to give up his throne. then the bolsheviks, a communist party led by vladimir lenin, seized power, promising "peace, land, and bread". they pulled russia out of the war.

a brutal civil war followed, which the communists won. the tsar and his family were executed, and the country became the soviet union (ussr), the world's first communist state. after lenin died, joseph stalin took control. he forced farmers onto collective farms, causing terrible famines, rapidly built up industry, and had millions of people killed or sent to prison labor camps called the gulag.

a troubled peace

at the peace conference in paris, the winners wrote the treaty of versailles. germany had to accept blame for the war, give up land and colonies, shrink its army, and pay huge sums of money called reparations. many germans felt humiliated and angry.

the empires of austria-hungary, the ottomans, germany, and russia all collapsed. new countries appeared in europe, such as poland, czechoslovakia, and yugoslavia. the ottoman lands in the middle east were split between britain and france. the league of nations was set up to keep peace, but it was weak, and the united states never joined.

after the war, many countries let women vote. in the ottoman empire's place, mustafa kemal atatürk founded modern turkey. in india, mahatma gandhi led a campaign of non-violent protest against british rule.

the great depression

after a boom, the american stock market crashed. banks failed, businesses closed, and the crisis spread around the world. this was the great depression. tens of millions of people lost their jobs, and many went hungry.

in the united states, president franklin d. roosevelt launched the new deal, using government programs to create jobs and help people. but in other countries, desperate people turned to extreme leaders who promised to fix everything.

the rise of dictators

in italy, benito mussolini founded fascism, a system that glorified the nation, war, and a single all-powerful leader, and crushed all opposition. in germany, adolf hitler and his nazi party came to power. hitler blamed germany's problems on jews, communists, and the treaty of versailles, and he dreamed of conquering land in eastern europe.

the nazis made hitler dictator, took away the rights of jewish people, and rebuilt the army. in asia, japan was ruled by military leaders who invaded manchuria and then launched a full war on china, committing terrible atrocities like the massacre at nanjing. britain and france tried to avoid war by letting hitler take austria and part of czechoslovakia, a policy called appeasement. it did not work.

the second world war

after making a secret deal with stalin, hitler invaded poland, and britain and france declared war. using "blitzkrieg", or lightning war, with tanks and planes, germany quickly conquered much of europe, including france. britain, led by winston churchill, fought on alone for a time, and won the battle of britain in the skies.

hitler then broke his deal and invaded the soviet union. the soviets suffered enormous losses, but at the battle of stalingrad they destroyed a whole german army, and began pushing germany back. japan attacked the american naval base at pearl harbor, bringing the united states into the war. the war was now truly global: the allies (britain, the soviet union, the united states, china, and others) against the axis (germany, italy, japan).

in the pacific, the americans beat japan at the battle of midway and then fought island by island toward japan. in europe, the allies invaded italy, and on d-day, a massive force landed on the beaches of normandy in france. germany was squeezed between the western allies and the soviets.

the holocaust

the nazis carried out the holocaust, the deliberate, organized murder of six million jews, about two out of every three jews in europe. they were rounded up, forced into ghettos, shot in mass killings, and sent to death camps like auschwitz, where they were murdered in gas chambers.

the nazis also murdered millions of others, including roma, disabled people, polish and soviet civilians, prisoners of war, gay people, and political opponents. the holocaust is one of the darkest crimes in human history. afterwards, the world created the word "genocide" and made it a crime under international law.

the end of the war

as soviet troops reached berlin, hitler killed himself, and germany surrendered. japan kept fighting. the united states then dropped two atomic bombs on the japanese cities of hiroshima and nagasaki, killing well over a hundred thousand people. the soviet union also declared war on japan, and japan surrendered.

the second world war was the deadliest conflict in history. somewhere around seventy to eighty-five million people died, most of them civilians. the soviet union and china suffered the most deaths. nazi and japanese leaders were put on trial for war crimes at nuremberg and tokyo. europe lay in ruins, and two superpowers, the united states and the soviet union, now faced each other. that leads to part 11.
