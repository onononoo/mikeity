part 11: the modern world

1945 – the present (about 81 years)

the cold war, the end of empires, the struggle for civil rights, space flight, the computer, and the internet shape the world we live in now, along with new dangers like climate change.

a new world

the second world war left a world transformed. europe was in ruins, and its empires were about to collapse. two superpowers, the united states and the soviet union, dominated. the atomic bomb had made a third world war potentially the end of civilization. over the following decades, dozens of new nations were born, the world economy grew faster than ever before, the population more than tripled, humans left the earth, and computers and the internet connected the planet. and people began to understand that human activity was changing the climate of the earth itself.

the united nations and a new order

in 1945, fifty countries signed the charter of the united nations, which aimed to prevent war and promote cooperation. its security council gave permanent seats and veto power to the five great powers of the war: the united states, the soviet union, britain, france, and china. in 1948, the un adopted the universal declaration of human rights, which states that all human beings are born free and equal in dignity and rights.

the united states, which had emerged from the war as by far the richest country, led the building of a new economic order, with the international monetary fund, the world bank, and agreements to lower trade barriers. through the marshall plan, it gave billions of dollars to rebuild western europe. west germany and japan were rebuilt as democracies and became economic powerhouses.

european countries, determined never to fight each other again, began tying their economies together, starting with coal and steel in 1951 and the treaty of rome in 1957. this grew into the european union, formally created in 1993, and a shared currency, the euro, in 1999.

the cold war

two superpowers

the wartime alliance between the united states and the soviet union broke down almost immediately. the united states was capitalist and democratic; the soviet union was communist and ruled by one party. the soviets installed communist governments across eastern europe. churchill said an "iron curtain" had come down across europe. the cold war had begun.

the united states adopted a policy of "containment": stopping the spread of communism wherever it appeared. in 1949, the western powers formed nato, a military alliance; the soviets later formed the warsaw pact. germany was divided into a democratic west and a communist east. in 1948-1949, when the soviets blockaded west berlin, the western allies supplied it entirely by air for almost a year.

the nuclear arms race

the soviet union tested its own atomic bomb in 1949. both sides then built hydrogen bombs, hundreds of times more powerful, and missiles that could hit the other side within half an hour. by the 1980s, there were about 70,000 nuclear warheads in the world. the idea of "mutually assured destruction", that any nuclear war would destroy both sides, may have helped keep the peace between the superpowers.

in 1962, the soviet union secretly placed nuclear missiles in cuba, just 150 kilometers from the united states. for thirteen days, during the cuban missile crisis, the world came closer to nuclear war than ever before. president john f. kennedy ordered a naval blockade; soviet leader nikita khrushchev finally agreed to remove the missiles in return for an american promise not to invade cuba and a secret deal to remove american missiles from turkey. afterwards, a telephone "hotline" was set up between washington and moscow.

the berlin wall

millions of east germans fled to the west through berlin. in 1961, the east german government built the berlin wall to stop them. it became the symbol of a divided world. at least 140 people were killed trying to cross it.

hot wars

the cold war was "cold" only between the superpowers. elsewhere, it was fought through wars that killed millions:

  • korea (1950-1953): communist north korea invaded the south. a un force led by the united states pushed them back; then china intervened. the war ended in stalemate, and korea is still divided.
  • vietnam (1955-1975): after vietnam won independence from france, it was divided. the united states sent hundreds of thousands of troops to stop the communist north from taking the south. despite massive bombing, it failed. the war killed perhaps 2 to 3 million vietnamese and about 58,000 americans, and deeply divided american society. vietnam was reunified under communist rule in 1975. in neighboring cambodia, the khmer rouge regime killed about a quarter of the population in 1975-1979.
  • afghanistan (1979-1989): the soviet union invaded to support a communist government and fought islamist guerrillas, the mujahideen, armed by the united states, pakistan, and saudi arabia. the soviets withdrew in defeat.
  • in latin america, africa, and asia, both superpowers backed coups, dictatorships, and rebel movements, often with devastating consequences.

communist china

after japan's defeat, the chinese civil war resumed between the nationalists of chiang kai-shek and the communists of mao zedong. the communists won, and in 1949 mao proclaimed the people's republic of china. the nationalists fled to the island of taiwan.

mao's rule transformed china, at a terrible cost. land was taken from landlords, many of whom were killed. in the great leap forward (1958-1962), mao tried to industrialize instantly, forcing peasants into huge communes and ordering backyard steel furnaces. the result was the deadliest famine in history, killing an estimated 15 to 45 million people. in the cultural revolution (1966-1976), mao unleashed young red guards against teachers, officials, and anyone accused of being "bourgeois". millions were persecuted, and much of china's cultural heritage was destroyed.

after mao's death, deng xiaoping launched "reform and opening" in 1978, allowing private farming and business and welcoming foreign investment, while keeping the communist party in total control. china's economy grew at around 10 percent a year for three decades, lifting hundreds of millions of people out of poverty. in 1989, the government sent the army to crush pro-democracy protests in and around tiananmen square in beijing, killing hundreds or perhaps thousands. by the 2010s, china had become the world's second largest economy and the largest manufacturer.

the end of empires

why empires fell

in 1945, much of africa and asia was still ruled by european empires. within about thirty years, almost all of it was independent. this decolonization happened because:

  • the war had weakened britain, france, and the netherlands economically and militarily;
  • nationalist movements had grown strong, and many colonial soldiers had fought for "freedom" in the war;
  • both superpowers, for their own reasons, opposed the old european empires;
  • the un charter spoke of the self-determination of peoples.

south asia

india won independence in 1947, after decades of struggle led by the indian national congress, mahatma gandhi, and jawaharlal nehru, who became its first prime minister. but british india was divided along religious lines into hindu-majority india and muslim-majority pakistan. the partition set off horrific violence: up to 15 million people fled across the new borders, and hundreds of thousands, perhaps up to two million, were killed. gandhi was assassinated in 1948 by a hindu nationalist. in 1971, east pakistan broke away, after a brutal war, to become bangladesh.

the middle east

in 1947, the un voted to divide british-ruled palestine into a jewish and an arab state. in 1948, the state of israel was declared, and neighboring arab states attacked. israel won, and about 700,000 palestinians fled or were expelled from their homes, an event palestinians call the nakba, "the catastrophe". further wars followed in 1956, 1967 (when israel occupied the west bank, gaza, the golan heights, and sinai), and 1973. the israeli-palestinian conflict remains unresolved. the discovery of vast oil reserves made the persian gulf region central to the world economy.

africa

in 1957, ghana, led by kwame nkrumah, became the first colony in sub-saharan africa to win independence. in 1960, the "year of africa", seventeen countries became independent. some colonies won freedom through negotiation. others had to fight: in algeria, a war of independence against france (1954-1962) killed hundreds of thousands. in kenya, britain crushed the mau mau uprising with detention camps and torture before granting independence in 1963. portugal held on to its colonies until 1975.

independence brought huge hope, but also great challenges: borders drawn by colonizers, economies built to export raw materials, few trained officials, and cold war interference. many countries suffered coups, one-party rule, and civil wars, like the nigerian civil war (1967-1970). others built stable institutions, and africa's population and cities have grown fast.

apartheid and its end

in south africa, the white minority government introduced apartheid in 1948: strict racial segregation and denial of political rights to the black majority. black south africans were forced into separate "homelands" and townships. nelson mandela and other leaders of the african national congress turned to armed struggle after peaceful protest was met with massacres, like at sharpeville in 1960. mandela was imprisoned for 27 years. international sanctions, boycotts, and constant resistance, especially by young people in the townships, pressured the government. mandela was released in 1990, and in 1994, in the country's first election open to all races, he became president. the country set up a truth and reconciliation commission to confront the crimes of the past.

the fight for equal rights

the american civil rights movement

in the united states, black americans in the south still faced segregation, denial of voting rights, and racist violence. in 1954, the supreme court ruled in brown v. board of education that segregated schools were unconstitutional. in 1955, rosa parks refused to give up her bus seat to a white passenger in montgomery, alabama, sparking a year-long bus boycott led by a young minister, martin luther king jr..

the civil rights movement used non-violent protest, sit-ins, freedom rides, marches, and voter registration drives, often met with violence by police and white supremacists. in 1963, at the march on washington, king gave his "i have a dream" speech. the civil rights act of 1964 banned segregation and discrimination, and the voting rights act of 1965 protected the right to vote. king was assassinated in 1968.

other movements

  • women's rights: the "second wave" of feminism, from the 1960s, campaigned for equal pay, equal opportunities, reproductive rights, and an end to discrimination and violence against women. in many countries women entered universities and professions in large numbers.
  • lgbtq rights: the stonewall uprising in new york in 1969 is often seen as the start of the modern gay rights movement. homosexuality was gradually decriminalized in many countries, and in 2001 the netherlands became the first to allow same-sex marriage. many countries, however, still criminalize it.
  • indigenous rights: indigenous peoples in the americas, australia, new zealand, and elsewhere campaigned for land rights, recognition, and self-government.
  • disability rights: campaigners won laws banning discrimination and requiring access.

the space race

the cold war rivals competed to show their technological superiority in space. on 4 october 1957, the soviet union launched sputnik 1, the first artificial satellite. in 1961, yuri gagarin became the first human in space. the united states responded with the apollo program, and on 20 july 1969, neil armstrong and buzz aldrin of apollo 11 became the first people to walk on the moon, while michael collins orbited above.

later came space stations, reusable space shuttles, the international space station (continuously crewed since 2000), telescopes like hubble and the james webb space telescope, and robotic explorers across the solar system, from rovers on mars to probes that have left the solar system entirely. satellites transformed communication, weather forecasting, and navigation.

science and medicine

  • in 1953, james watson and francis crick, using data including x-ray images made by rosalind franklin, described the double helix structure of dna. in 2003, the human genome project finished reading the human genome.
  • vaccines drove down deadly diseases like polio and measles, and in 1980 smallpox became the first human disease to be eradicated.
  • antibiotics, safer surgery, organ transplants, and better public health helped global life expectancy rise from about 46 years in 1950 to over 70 today.
  • the "green revolution" of new high-yield crops, fertilizers, and irrigation, from the 1960s, greatly increased food production in asia and latin america.
  • aids, first identified in 1981, killed tens of millions before effective treatments were developed.

the end of the cold war

by the 1980s, the soviet economy was stagnating, weighed down by military spending and inefficient central planning. in 1985, a new leader, mikhail gorbachev, introduced glasnost ("openness") and perestroika ("restructuring"). he signed arms control treaties with the united states and pulled soviet troops out of afghanistan.

when gorbachev made clear that the soviet army would no longer prop up communist governments in eastern europe, they fell one after another in 1989, mostly peacefully. in poland, the solidarity trade union won free elections. on 9 november 1989, crowds broke through the berlin wall. germany was reunited in 1990. in december 1991, the soviet union itself dissolved into fifteen independent countries. the cold war was over.

the break-up was not always peaceful. yugoslavia collapsed into a series of wars in the 1990s, including the genocide of bosnian muslims at srebrenica in 1995.

a global world

globalization

after the cold war, globalization accelerated. container shipping, cheap air travel, and the internet tied the world's economies together. china, india, and other countries opened their economies and grew rapidly, and extreme poverty fell dramatically: from about half of the world's population in 1950 to under 10 percent. but inequality within many countries grew, and factory jobs moved from rich countries to poorer ones.

conflict and crisis

  • in 1994, in rwanda, hutu extremists murdered about 800,000 people, mostly tutsi, in about 100 days, while the world failed to intervene.
  • on 11 september 2001, al-qaeda terrorists hijacked four airliners and attacked new york and washington, killing nearly 3,000 people. the united states and its allies invaded afghanistan in 2001 and iraq in 2003, starting long wars. the taliban returned to power in afghanistan in 2021.
  • in 2008, a financial crisis that started in the american housing market caused the worst global recession since the great depression.
  • from 2010-2011, the "arab spring" uprisings toppled several dictators in the middle east and north africa, but syria, libya, and yemen fell into civil war. the syrian war forced more than half of syria's population from their homes.
  • in 2014 russia annexed crimea, and in 2022 it launched a full-scale invasion of ukraine, the largest war in europe since 1945.

computers and the internet

the first electronic computers, built during and after the second world war, filled rooms. the invention of the transistor in 1947 and the integrated circuit, or microchip, around 1958-1959 made computers smaller, faster, and cheaper. the number of transistors on a chip doubled roughly every two years for decades, a pattern known as moore's law.

the internet grew from arpanet, a network funded by the us military, which sent its first message in 1969. in 1989, tim berners-lee, working at cern in switzerland, proposed the world wide web, a way of linking documents using hypertext; it was opened to the public in 1991. websites, search engines, email, and online shopping spread through the 1990s. in the 2000s came social media and smartphones, putting the internet in billions of pockets. by the 2020s, about two thirds of the world's population used the internet, and artificial intelligence programs that can write, translate, and generate images became widely used.

the world today

people

the world's population passed 8 billion in 2022, up from about 2.5 billion in 1950. more than half of all people now live in cities. people live longer, healthier lives than at any point in the past, and more can read and write than ever before. birth rates are falling in most of the world, and many countries have aging populations.

the planet

since the industrial revolution, burning coal, oil, and gas has raised the level of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere by about 50 percent, warming the planet. average global temperatures have risen by more than 1 degree celsius, and 2024 was the hottest year on record so far. the effects include melting glaciers and ice sheets, rising seas, more extreme heat waves, droughts, floods, and fires, and threats to ecosystems. in the 2015 paris agreement, nearly every country agreed to try to limit warming to well below 2 degrees. the environmentalist wangari maathai, whose green belt movement planted tens of millions of trees in kenya, was one of many who showed how environmental protection and human rights are linked.

many scientists now speak of a new geological age, the "anthropocene", in which humans are the main force changing the planet. species are going extinct far faster than normal, leading some scientists to warn of a sixth mass extinction.

pandemic

in 2020, a new coronavirus, which causes covid-19, spread around the world. countries closed borders, schools, and businesses. vaccines were developed in under a year, much faster than ever before, but the pandemic killed millions.

history goes on

this site started with the big bang and ends with today. the next part of the story has not been written yet. it will be made by everyone alive now.

how we know

  • an enormous volume of records, media, and data: government archives, newspapers, television, and now digital records of almost everything.
  • declassified documents, such as cold war files released decades later.
  • oral history from people who lived through events.
  • satellite data, climate records, and scientific measurements.
  • the challenge for historians of recent times is not too little evidence but too much, and deciding which of it matters.

questions scholars still argue about

  • why did the cold war end peacefully? the role of gorbachev, reagan, the soviet economy, popular protest, or nuclear deterrence?
  • has globalization made the world better or worse? it has lifted billions out of poverty, but has also brought inequality and instability.
  • why have some former colonies prospered while others have struggled?
  • is the anthropocene a real new geological age? in 2024, geologists voted not to make it formally official, but the debate continues.
  • what should we call our own time? it is always hardest to see the shape of the present.

key terms

apartheid
the system of racial segregation and white minority rule enforced by law in south africa from 1948 to the early 1990s.
big bang
the rapid expansion of the universe from an extremely hot and dense early state, about 13.8 billion years ago.
cold war
the long rivalry between the united states and the soviet union after the second world war, fought through arms races, spying, and wars in other countries rather than directly.
decolonization
the process by which colonies became independent countries, especially after 1945.
dna
the molecule that carries the genetic instructions of living things. ancient dna lets scientists trace how people moved and mixed.
extinction
the dying out of a species. a mass extinction is when a large share of all species die out in a short time.
genome
the complete set of dna in a living thing.
glasnost
"openness": gorbachev's policy of allowing more free discussion in the soviet union.
globalization
the growing connection of the world's economies, cultures, and peoples through trade, travel, and communication.
pandemic
an outbreak of disease that spreads across many countries or the whole world.
partition
the division of a territory into separate states, especially the 1947 division of british india into india and pakistan.
perestroika
"restructuring": gorbachev's program of reforms to the soviet economy and government.
segregation
the forced separation of people by race in schools, housing, transport, and public places.
