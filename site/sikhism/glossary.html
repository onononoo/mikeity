sikhism: glossary

words used in sikhism (18 terms). words linked in the story point here. the main glossary has the rest.

a · f · g · h · i · k · l · m · n · p · s · v · w

a

amrit
"nectar": the sweetened water used when a sikh is initiated into the khalsa. see also: khalsa.

f

five ks
the five signs worn by initiated sikhs: kesh (uncut hair), kara (steel bracelet), kanga (comb), kachera (cotton undershorts), and kirpan (small sword). see also: khalsa.

g

gurdwara
"the guru's door": a sikh place of worship, where the guru granth sahib is kept and the langar is served. see also: langar.
gurmukhi
"from the guru's mouth": the script in which the punjabi language and the guru granth sahib are written.
guru
"teacher": in sikhism, the ten human gurus from nanak to gobind singh, and after them the scripture itself. see also: guru granth sahib.
guru granth sahib
the sikh scripture, with hymns by six gurus and by hindu and muslim saints, treated as the living guru. see also: guru.

h

hukam
the will or order of god, by which everything happens.

i

ik onkar
"there is one god": the first words of the guru granth sahib, and the symbol of sikhism.

k

khalsa
the community of initiated sikhs, founded by guru gobind singh in 1699. see also: five ks, amrit.
kirtan
singing the hymns of the scripture to music, the heart of sikh worship.

l

langar
the free kitchen in every gurdwara, where all sit together and eat the same food, whatever their caste or faith. see also: seva.

m

miri piri
worldly and spiritual power, shown by guru hargobind's two swords.

n

naam simran
remembering god by repeating and meditating on god's name. see also: waheguru.

p

panj pyare
"the five beloved ones": the first five sikhs initiated into the khalsa in 1699. see also: khalsa.

s

sangat
the congregation of sikhs gathered in worship.
seva
selfless service to others, without thought of reward. see also: langar.

v

vaisakhi
the spring harvest festival, on which the khalsa was founded in 1699.

w

waheguru
"wonderful lord": the name sikhs most often use for god. see also: naam simran.
