sikhism

devotion to the one creator, remembered through god's name, honest work, and sharing with others. it teaches that all people are equal, and it was shaped by ten gurus over two centuries.

beganc. 1500 ce, in the punjab
founder or first teachersguru nanak, followed by nine more human gurus
scripturesthe guru granth sahib, treated as the living guru
holy placesthe harmandir sahib (golden temple) in amritsar, and the five takhts
followers todayabout 25 to 30 million, most in the punjab
how the world beganbefore creation there was only the one, formless and in deep stillness. by god's will, the hukam, the universe came to be, with countless worlds and skies.
how old the world isguru nanak taught that only the creator knows when the world was made: no scholar, holy book, or calendar can say. many sikhs see science and scripture as describing the same vast creation.

the one, before all things

the guru granth sahib begins with ik onkar, "there is one god". god is the creator, the truth, without fear, without hate, beyond time, never born and self-existent. before creation, in the words of guru nanak, "for endless ages there was only darkness": no earth or sky, no day or night, no sun or moon, only the one in deep, formless stillness.

by the hukam, god's will, the universe came into being. "with one word you made the vast expanse," guru nanak sang, "and hundreds of thousands of rivers began to flow." there are worlds beneath worlds, and hundreds of thousands of skies above; people have grown tired of searching for their limits.

and when was the world made? guru nanak asked the question himself: "what was that time, what was that moment, what day, what date ... when the world was made?" the scholars do not know, nor the writers of holy books, nor the yogis. "the creator who made the world knows alone." many sikhs see the vast universe described by science as a sign of the same greatness.

the first guru

guru nanak was born in 1469 in the punjab, then ruled by muslim sultans, into a hindu family. as a young man he worked as a storekeeper and spent his free time in prayer with his muslim friend and musician, bhai mardana. according to tradition, one morning he went into the river bein to bathe and disappeared for three days. when he came back, he said: "there is no hindu, there is no muslim", meaning that god sees people, not labels.

with mardana he made long journeys to teach, and his hymns were sung to music, as kirtan still is. in about 1521 he settled at kartarpur, where his followers lived as farmers, prayed together, and ate together. he taught three simple duties: remember god's name (naam simran), earn an honest living, and share with others.

ten gurus

before he died in 1539, guru nanak passed his light to a disciple, guru angad, who spread the gurmukhi script, while his wife mata khivi ran the langar. guru amar das insisted that everyone eat together, rich or poor, before they could see him, and guru ram das founded amritsar. guru arjan gathered the hymns of the gurus, and of hindu and muslim saints, into one book and built the harmandir sahib with doors on all four sides, open to all. in 1606 the mughal emperor had him put to death.

after that, guru hargobind wore two swords, for spiritual and worldly power, miri piri. guru tegh bahadur was executed in 1675 for standing up for the right of hindus to practice their faith. in 1699 guru gobind singh, the tenth guru, called the sikhs together at vaisakhi and founded the khalsa, initiating the panj pyare with amrit and giving them the five ks. before his death in 1708 he declared that there would be no more human gurus: the scripture itself would be the guru forever.

what historians can show

unlike older faiths, sikhism began in well-recorded times, and the basic story of the ten gurus is not in doubt. the stories of guru nanak's life, the janamsakhis, were written down decades after his death and include miracles; historians treat the details with care, but his hymns survive in the scripture in his own words.

empire and partition

after the gurus, sikhs fought mughal and afghan armies. banda singh bahadur led an uprising, and later ranjit singh built a sikh empire in the punjab and covered the harmandir sahib in gold, making it the golden temple. the british conquered the punjab in 1849, and in 1919 their troops massacred hundreds at amritsar. partition in 1947 split the punjab in two, and in 1984 the indian army stormed the golden temple, followed by the killing of thousands of sikhs. today about 25 to 30 million sikhs live around the world.

belief and practice

  • one god, waheguru, who is in everything and everyone.
  • all people are equal, whatever their caste, sex, or religion.
  • worship in the gurdwara with the sangat, and seva, selfless service.
  • the langar, a free meal for anyone who comes.
  • the goal: to live free of ego and to merge with god, like a drop of water in the ocean.
