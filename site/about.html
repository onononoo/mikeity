what this site is

the history of the world tries to tell the whole story, from the big bang to the present, in eleven parts. it is a starting point, not an encyclopedia: every topic here has whole libraries written about it. if something catches your interest, go and find out more.

each part has the same shape: the story itself, a section on how we know (the evidence historians and scientists use), a section on questions scholars still argue about, a table of dated events, the people involved, and the key terms. the first part starts at the very beginning.

how to read dates

written like thismeans
13.8 billion years agodeep time, counted back from the present
c. 3100 bceabout 3100 years before the common era ("c." means approximately)
476 ce476 years into the common era. "ce" is left off for years after 1000
1914–1918a range of years
5th century bcethe years 500 to 401 bce

"bce" (before the common era) and "ce" (common era) mean the same as the older "bc" and "ad". the timeline filter and the search box understand all of these forms.

how it is made

the site is built by a small program written for it, using several languages:

languagewhat it doeswhere
pythonreads the data, parses dates and markup, fills templates, writes every page, and checks the resultbuilder/
jsonholds the events, people, glossary, regions, and partsdata/
plain textthe story of each part, in a tiny markup languagecontent/
htmlthe page templatestemplates/

javascriptsorting tables, filtering the timeline, and search, in the browserstatic/js/
javascript (node)checks that every link and anchor on the finished site workstools/checklinks.js
c++draws the charts on the statistics page as svg picturestools/chart.cpp
awkfinds the quietest stretches and the busiest years on the timelinetools/gaps.awk
c#works out which people were alive at the same timetools/contemporaries.cs
ca command line tool that prints the timeline as text, filtered by yearstools/timeline.c
perlcounts words, links, and sections in the content filestools/stats.pl
shell, powershell, and makerun all the steps in orderbuild.sh, build.ps1, makefile

every page is plain html. there are no cookies, no tracking, no ads, and nothing is loaded from other websites. the pages work without javascript; with it, you also get sorting, filtering, and search.

the finished site also includes its raw data: all of it as json, the timeline as tab-separated text, and a sqlite database you can query yourself.

a note on fairness

history is huge, messy, and argued about. this site tries to cover the whole world, not just one region, and to be honest about cruelty and injustice as well as achievement. a lot is left out simply to keep it readable. dates for early periods are often estimates, and new discoveries change what we know all the time. where scholars disagree, the site tries to say so.
