judaism

the religion of the jewish people: one god, who made a covenant with abraham and gave the torah to moses at sinai. it is the oldest of the three abrahamic faiths, and the root of christianity and islam.

beganc. 1000-500 bce, in the land of israel; by tradition with abraham
founder or first teachersabraham, by tradition; moses, who received the torah
scripturesthe hebrew bible (tanakh), above all the torah; the talmud
holy placesjerusalem, above all the western wall of the temple mount
followers todayabout 15 million
how the world begangod created the heavens and the earth in six days and rested on the seventh, as told in the book of genesis.
how old the world isthe jewish calendar counts years from creation, which rabbinic tradition places in 3761 bce. many jews today read the six days as a teaching, not a clock, and accept the scientific age.

in the beginning

the hebrew bible opens: "in the beginning god created the heavens and the earth." over six days god makes light, sky, sea and land, plants, sun and moon, fish and birds, animals, and finally humans, "in his image". on the seventh day god rests, and blesses it; this is the origin of the sabbath.

god forms adam from the dust of the earth and makes eve, and places them in the garden of eden. they eat the fruit god forbade and are sent out into the world. their descendants grow wicked, and god sends a flood, saving only noah, his family, and the animals in the ark. afterward god makes a covenant with noah and all living things, sealed with the rainbow.

by the traditional count, used in the jewish calendar, creation took place in 3761 bce. many jews read the six days as a way of teaching that the world is ordered and good, not as a measure of time, and see no conflict with the scientific age of the universe.

the covenant

god calls abraham to leave his home and go to canaan, and promises that his descendants will be a great nation and a blessing to the world. the promise passes to isaac, then to jacob, who is renamed israel. jacob's twelve sons become the twelve tribes. famine brings them to egypt, where joseph has risen to power, but later pharaohs enslave them.

moses leads the israelites out of egypt in the exodus, remembered every year at passover. at mount sinai, god gives moses the torah, with its ten commandments and hundreds of other laws, and the whole people accept the covenant. after forty years in the desert, they enter the promised land.

kings and prophets

the israelites are ruled by judges, then kings. david makes jerusalem his capital, and his son solomon builds the temple there. after solomon, the kingdom splits in two. prophets like elijah, isaiah, and jeremiah warn the people and their kings to keep the covenant and to do justice for the poor. the bible sees the fall of the kingdoms and the exile to babylon as the result of breaking the covenant, and the return as a sign of god's faithfulness.

what historians can show

outside the bible, the first mention of israel is on an egyptian stone of about 1208 bce. archaeology suggests that the israelites emerged mostly from peoples already living in the hills of canaan. an inscription of the 800s bce mentions the "house of david". the northern kingdom fell to assyria in 722 bce, and babylon destroyed jerusalem and its temple in 587 bce. in exile and after the return, many scholars think, the books of the torah were brought together into their final form.

the second temple stood for nearly six hundred years. in 70 ce the romans destroyed it, and after a last revolt most jews lived in the diaspora, scattered across the mediterranean, the middle east, and later europe.

the religion of the book

without a temple, judaism was rebuilt around the synagogue, prayer, and study, led by the rabbis. sages like hillel and rabbi akiva had already taught that the written torah came with an oral law. around 200 ce judah ha-nasi wrote it down in the mishnah, and over the next three centuries rabbis in babylon and the land of israel debated it in the talmud. in the middle ages, rashi explained the texts line by line and maimonides set out jewish law and philosophy.

jewish communities often flourished, in baghdad, muslim spain, and poland, but they also faced expulsions, massacres, and forced conversions. in the 1700s, hasidism brought a new warmth to prayer, and moses mendelssohn led jews into modern european culture.

the modern age

in the late 1800s, persecution in russia sent millions of jews abroad, and theodor herzl founded political zionism. between 1941 and 1945, the holocaust murdered six million jews, among them anne frank. in 1948 the state of israel was founded. today about fifteen million jews live around the world, most in israel and the united states, and they range from strictly orthodox to reform and secular.

belief and practice

  • one god, who created the world and made a covenant with israel.
  • the torah as god's teaching, studied and debated in every generation.
  • the sabbath and festivals such as passover, yom kippur, and hanukkah.
  • laws for daily life, like eating kosher food, and coming of age at a bar mitzvah.
  • hope for the messiah and for the repair of the world.
