judaism: glossary

words used in judaism (17 terms). words linked in the story point here. the main glossary has the rest.

b · c · d · h · k · m · p · r · s · t · y · z

b

bar mitzvah
the coming of age of a jewish boy at thirteen, when he becomes responsible for keeping the commandments. for girls it is the bat mitzvah, at twelve or thirteen.

c

covenant
a binding agreement between god and people: with noah, with abraham, and with all israel at sinai. see also: torah.

d

diaspora
the jewish communities living outside the land of israel, since the babylonian exile and above all since 70 ce.

h

hanukkah
the eight-day festival of lights, remembering the rededication of the temple by the maccabees in 164 bce.
hasidism
a movement begun by the baal shem tov in the 1700s, stressing joyful prayer and devotion, led by rebbes.

k

kosher
food allowed by jewish law: certain animals only, slaughtered in a set way, with meat and milk kept apart.

m

messiah
"the anointed one": a future king descended from david who, jews believe, will bring an age of peace. see also: covenant.
mishnah
the first written collection of the oral law, compiled around 200 ce. see also: talmud.

p

passover
the spring festival remembering the exodus from egypt, with a special meal called the seder.

r

rabbi
"my teacher": a scholar of jewish law who teaches and leads a community. see also: synagogue.

s

sabbath
shabbat: the day of rest from friday evening to saturday evening, remembering that god rested on the seventh day.
synagogue
a jewish house of prayer, study, and gathering. see also: rabbi.

t

talmud
the vast collection of rabbinic discussion of the mishnah, the foundation of jewish law. see also: mishnah.
tanakh
the hebrew bible: the torah (teaching), the nevi'im (prophets), and the ketuvim (writings). see also: torah.
torah
the five books of moses, from genesis to deuteronomy; in a wider sense, all jewish teaching. see also: tanakh.

y

yom kippur
the day of atonement, the holiest day of the year, spent in fasting and prayer.

z

zionism
the movement for a jewish homeland in the land of israel, which led to the founding of the state of israel in 1948.
