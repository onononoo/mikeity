religions

every religion has its own account of how the world began, what has happened in it, and where it is going. this section gives each of 6 major faiths a small site of its own, with its story, a timeline, its people, and a glossary, laid out just like the rest of this site.

each timeline mixes two kinds of entry, and always says which is which:

  • tradition: the story as the faith itself tells it, in its scriptures and teachings. dates given here are traditional ones.
  • history: what historians and archaeologists can show from evidence, like the rest of this site's timeline.

the site does not take sides. believers read many of the traditional accounts in different ways, some literally and some not, and the pages try to say so.

how old is the world?

the same question, answered by science and by each tradition.

according tohow the world beganhow old it is
sciencethe big bang: space, time, and matter began expanding from a hot, dense state. the earth formed from dust around the young sun.the universe is about 13.8 billion years old; the earth about 4.54 billion.
judaismgod created the heavens and the earth in six days and rested on the seventh, as told in the book of genesis.the jewish calendar counts years from creation, which rabbinic tradition places in 3761 bce. many jews today read the six days as a teaching, not a clock, and accept the scientific age.
christianitythe same account as the hebrew bible: god made the world in six days and made humans in his image. the gospel of john adds that all things were made through the word, which christians identify with jesus.the bible gives no date. in 1650 archbishop james ussher added up its lifespans and got 4004 bce, a reckoning that was widely printed. most churches today accept the scientific age of the earth; some christians hold to a young earth.
islamgod created the heavens and the earth in six days, commanding "be", and it was. he made adam from clay, breathed his spirit into him, and made him his deputy on earth.the quran gives no date. many scholars note that a "day" with god can be like a thousand or fifty thousand years, and many muslims accept the scientific age. the islamic calendar counts years from the hijra in 622 ce.
hinduismthe universe is created, sustained, and dissolved again and again, without beginning. in one telling, brahma the creator is born on a lotus from the navel of vishnu. an old hymn of the rigveda asks who really knows how it began.time runs in vast cycles. one day of brahma, a kalpa, lasts 4.32 billion years, close to the scientific age of the earth. we now live in the kali yuga, the last and darkest of four ages, which tradition says began in 3102 bce.
buddhismthe buddha set aside the question of how the world began as not helpful for ending suffering. buddhist texts describe countless world systems that form, last, decay, and form again.time has no known beginning. it is measured in kalpas, eons so long that a mountain wiped with a silk cloth once a century would wear away first. many buddhas have come in past eons, and one, maitreya, is still to come.
sikhismbefore creation there was only the one, formless and in deep stillness. by god's will, the hukam, the universe came to be, with countless worlds and skies.guru nanak taught that only the creator knows when the world was made: no scholar, holy book, or calendar can say. many sikhs see science and scripture as describing the same vast creation.

in short

judaism
the religion of the jewish people: one god, who made a covenant with abraham and gave the torah to moses at sinai. it is the oldest of the three abrahamic faiths, and the root of christianity and islam. (32 timeline entries, 22 people, 17 terms.)
christianity
faith in jesus of nazareth as the son of god and savior, who died on the cross and rose from the dead. it began as a movement within judaism and became the religion of the roman empire and then of much of the world. (33 timeline entries, 19 people, 19 terms.)
islam
submission (islam) to the one god, allah, as revealed in the quran to the prophet muhammad. muslims see it as the same faith taught by every prophet from adam, through abraham, moses, and jesus, to muhammad. (31 timeline entries, 21 people, 23 terms.)
hinduism
the family of traditions of india, called by many followers sanatana dharma, "the eternal way". it includes many gods seen as forms of one reality, and belief in karma, rebirth, and liberation. (25 timeline entries, 18 people, 18 terms.)
buddhism
the path taught by the buddha to end suffering, through understanding its causes, living ethically, and training the mind. it does not depend on a creator god. (30 timeline entries, 19 people, 19 terms.)
sikhism
devotion to the one creator, remembered through god's name, honest work, and sharing with others. it teaches that all people are equal, and it was shaped by ten gurus over two centuries. (26 timeline entries, 15 people, 18 terms.)
