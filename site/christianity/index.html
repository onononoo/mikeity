christianity

faith in jesus of nazareth as the son of god and savior, who died on the cross and rose from the dead. it began as a movement within judaism and became the religion of the roman empire and then of much of the world.

beganc. 30 ce, in judea
founder or first teachersjesus of nazareth, and his apostles
scripturesthe bible: the old testament (the hebrew bible) and the new testament
holy placesjerusalem, bethlehem, rome, and many shrines of saints
followers todayabout 2.4 billion, the largest religion in the world
how the world beganthe same account as the hebrew bible: god made the world in six days and made humans in his image. the gospel of john adds that all things were made through the word, which christians identify with jesus.
how old the world isthe bible gives no date. in 1650 archbishop james ussher added up its lifespans and got 4004 bce, a reckoning that was widely printed. most churches today accept the scientific age of the earth; some christians hold to a young earth.

creation and the fall

christianity shares the first part of its bible, the old testament, with judaism. it begins with god creating the world in six days and making humans in his image. the gospel of john adds a christian reading: "in the beginning was the word ... all things were made through him", and the word is jesus.

in the garden of eden, adam and eve disobey god. christians call this the fall: through it sin and death came into the world, and every human life since has been wounded by it. the rest of the bible is read as the story of how god sets out to save the world he made.

the bible gives no date for creation. in 1650 the irish archbishop james ussher added up its genealogies and arrived at 4004 bce, and his date was printed in the margins of english bibles for two centuries. today most churches, including the catholic church, accept the scientific age of the universe and evolution, while some christians hold that the earth is only thousands of years old.

the story of jesus

according to the four gospels, the angel gabriel told mary, a young woman of nazareth, that she would bear the son of god. jesus of nazareth was born in bethlehem. as a man he was baptized by john the baptist, chose twelve apostles, healed the sick, and taught about the kingdom of god, love of enemies, and care for the poor.

he went to jerusalem at passover, shared a last supper with his disciples, remembered in the eucharist, and was arrested, tried, and crucified by the romans. on the third day, christians believe, he rose from the dead, and mary magdalene was the first to see him. this resurrection, celebrated at easter, is the center of christian faith. fifty days later, at pentecost, the holy spirit came upon the apostles and they began to preach.

christians believe jesus is both fully god and fully human (the incarnation), and that god is one god in three persons, father, son, and holy spirit (the trinity).

what historians can show

almost all historians agree that jesus was a real jewish teacher from galilee, baptized by john, who was crucified under pontius pilate around 30 ce. roman and jewish writers, including tacitus and josephus, mention him. his birth is usually dated a few years before the year 1, which was fixed by a monk in the 500s who miscounted slightly. the oldest christian writings are the letters of paul of tarsus, from the 50s ce; the gospels followed between about 65 and 100 ce.

from outlaws to empire

the first christians were jews, but paul of tarsus and others spread the faith among gentiles across the roman empire. christians were persecuted at times, and by tradition peter and paul died in rome under nero. in 313 constantine made christianity legal, and in 325 the council of nicaea agreed the creed still said in most churches. by 380 it was the religion of the empire. jerome translated the bible into latin, and augustine of hippo wrote the books that shaped western thought for a thousand years.

east and west

after rome fell in the west, monks following the rule of benedict of nursia kept learning alive in their monasteries and carried the faith to northern europe. the church divided in 1054 between the catholic west, led by the pope, and the orthodox east. the middle ages saw great cathedrals, universities, the crusades, francis of assisi, and thomas aquinas.

in 1517 martin luther challenged the church, and the reformation split western christianity. protestant churches followed luther, john calvin, and others; the catholic church answered with the council of trent and new orders like the jesuits of ignatius of loyola. wars of religion followed. later, john wesley and others led revivals that spread across britain and america.

a world faith

missionaries, colonial empires, and later local preachers carried christianity to the americas, africa, and asia. it also inspired movements against slavery and for civil rights, like that of martin luther king jr., and figures like mother teresa and desmond tutu. today there are about 2.4 billion christians, and most of them live in the global south: brazil, mexico, nigeria, the philippines, and congo have some of the largest christian populations on earth.

belief and practice

  • one god in three persons, and jesus as savior, crucified and risen.
  • the bible, read in church and at home.
  • baptism, the eucharist, and other sacraments.
  • the great feasts of christmas, easter, and pentecost, and honor for the saints.
  • the hope that christ will come again, raise the dead, and make all things new.
