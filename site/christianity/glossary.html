christianity: glossary

words used in christianity (19 terms). words linked in the story point here. the main glossary has the rest.

a · b · c · e · g · i · m · o · p · r · s · t

a

apostle
"one who is sent": the twelve closest followers of jesus, and early missionaries like paul.

b

baptism
the washing with water by which a person becomes a christian. see also: sacrament.
bible
the christian scriptures: the old testament, shared with judaism, and the new testament, about jesus and the early church. see also: gospel.

c

catholic
"universal": the church led by the pope in rome, the largest christian church. see also: pope.
christmas
the feast of the birth of jesus, on 25 december (7 january in many orthodox churches).
creed
a short statement of belief, like the nicene creed of 325.

e

easter
the feast of the resurrection of jesus, the most important day of the christian year. see also: resurrection.
eucharist
holy communion or the mass: sharing bread and wine as jesus did at the last supper. see also: sacrament.

g

gospel
"good news": the message of jesus, and the four books of matthew, mark, luke, and john that tell his life. see also: bible.

i

incarnation
the belief that god became a human being in jesus. see also: trinity.

m

monastery
a community of monks or nuns living under a rule of prayer and work.

o

orthodox church
the eastern churches, such as the greek and russian, which split from rome in 1054.

p

pentecost
the feast fifty days after easter, remembering the holy spirit coming down on the apostles.
pope
the bishop of rome and head of the catholic church, seen as the successor of peter. see also: catholic.
protestant
the churches that came out of the reformation, such as lutheran, reformed, anglican, baptist, and methodist, and later pentecostal.

r

resurrection
jesus rising from the dead on the third day after his crucifixion; also the rising of all the dead at the end of time. see also: easter.

s

sacrament
a holy rite through which, christians believe, god gives grace, such as baptism and the eucharist. see also: baptism, eucharist.
saint
a holy person; in the catholic and orthodox churches, someone officially recognized as being in heaven.

t

trinity
the belief that the one god is three persons: father, son, and holy spirit. see also: incarnation.
