glossary

short explanations of the harder words used on this site (123 terms). words linked in the text of each part point here.

a · b · c · d · e · f · g · h · i · j · l · m · n · o · p · r · s · t · v · z

a

abolitionism
the movement to end slavery and the slave trade. see also: slavery. used in: part 8.
absolutism
a system in which a monarch holds total power, not limited by a parliament or written laws. see also: divine right of kings. used in: part 7.
agricultural revolution
the change from hunting and gathering to farming, which happened on its own in several parts of the world after the last ice age. also called the neolithic revolution. see also: domestication, neolithic. used in: part 3.
alphabet
a writing system with a small set of letters that stand for individual sounds, rather than whole words or syllables. see also: cuneiform, hieroglyphs. used in: part 4, part 5.
amniote
an animal whose eggs have a waterproof membrane, letting them develop on dry land. reptiles, birds, and mammals are all amniotes. used in: part 1.
apartheid
the system of racial segregation and white minority rule enforced by law in south africa from 1948 to the early 1990s. see also: segregation. used in: part 11.
appeasement
giving in to an aggressive power's demands in the hope of avoiding war, as britain and france did with hitler in the 1930s. used in: part 10.
aqueduct
a channel or bridge built to carry water over long distances, especially the great stone ones built by the romans. used in: part 5.
archaeology
the study of the past through the things people left behind, such as tools, buildings, bones, and rubbish.
armistice
an agreement to stop fighting, which is not yet a full peace treaty. used in: part 10.
australopithecus
a group of early human relatives from africa who walked upright but had brains about the size of a chimpanzee's. see also: hominin. used in: part 2.
axial age
a name some historians give to the period when great thinkers in china, india, persia, israel, and greece laid the foundations of many later religions and philosophies. used in: part 5.

b

big bang
the rapid expansion of the universe from an extremely hot and dense early state, about 13.8 billion years ago. used in: part 1, part 11.
bolsheviks
the radical wing of russia's marxist party, led by lenin, which seized power in 1917 and later became the communist party. see also: communism. used in: part 10.
bronze age
the period when bronze, an alloy of copper and tin, was the main metal for tools and weapons. it began at different times in different places. see also: iron age. used in: part 3.
bronze age collapse
the fall of several great civilizations of the eastern mediterranean and near east around 1200-1150 bce. used in: part 4, part 5.

c

caliph
a successor to muhammad as the political and religious leader of the muslim community. a caliph's state is a caliphate. used in: part 6.
capitalism
an economic system in which businesses and property are privately owned and goods are traded in markets for profit. see also: socialism, communism. used in: part 8.
caste
a system of hereditary social groups, especially in south asia, that shaped whom people could marry and what work they did. used in: part 4.
city-state
a city that rules itself and the land around it as an independent state, like athens or ur. see also: polis. used in: part 4.
civilization
a complex society with cities, a government, specialized jobs, social classes, monumental building, and usually writing. used in: part 4.
cold war
the long rivalry between the united states and the soviet union after the second world war, fought through arms races, spying, and wars in other countries rather than directly. used in: part 11.
colonialism
one country taking control of another land and its people, usually settling it or exploiting its resources. see also: imperialism, decolonization.
columbian exchange
the huge transfer of plants, animals, people, and diseases between the americas and the rest of the world after 1492. used in: part 7.
communism
the idea of a classless society where property is shared. in practice, the one-party states that followed marx and lenin. see also: socialism, capitalism. used in: part 9.
conquistador
a spanish or portuguese soldier and adventurer who took part in the conquest of the americas.
counter-reformation
the catholic church's response to the protestant reformation, reforming itself and fighting protestantism. see also: reformation. used in: part 7.
crusades
a series of religious wars called by the popes, above all to take and hold jerusalem, from 1095 on. used in: part 6.
cuneiform
the wedge-shaped writing pressed into clay tablets in mesopotamia, the oldest known writing system. see also: hieroglyphs. used in: part 4.
cyanobacteria
microbes that make food from sunlight and release oxygen. they changed earth's atmosphere billions of years ago. used in: part 1.

d

decolonization
the process by which colonies became independent countries, especially after 1945. see also: colonialism. used in: part 11.
democracy
rule by the people, either directly (as in ancient athens) or through elected representatives. used in: part 5.
divine right of kings
the belief that kings get their authority directly from god and answer to no one on earth. see also: absolutism. used in: part 7.
dna
the molecule that carries the genetic instructions of living things. ancient dna lets scientists trace how people moved and mixed. see also: genome. used in: part 1, part 2, part 3, part 4, part 6, part 11.
domestication
changing wild plants or animals over generations, by choosing which ones to breed, so that they depend on and serve humans. used in: part 3.
dynasty
a series of rulers from the same family.

e

empire
a large state that rules over many different peoples and territories, usually gained by conquest.
enlightenment
an intellectual movement that stressed reason, science, and individual rights, and questioned the power of kings and churches. used in: part 7, part 8.
eukaryote
a living thing whose cells have a nucleus. all animals, plants, and fungi are eukaryotes. used in: part 1.
evolution
the change in living things over many generations. charles darwin explained it by natural selection. see also: natural selection. used in: part 1.
extinction
the dying out of a species. a mass extinction is when a large share of all species die out in a short time. used in: part 1, part 11.

f

fascism
a far-right political movement that glorifies the nation and war, crushes opposition, and demands obedience to one leader. see also: totalitarianism. used in: part 10.
fertile crescent
an arc of good farmland in the middle east, from the persian gulf up through iraq and syria and down to the mediterranean coast, where farming first began. used in: part 3.
feudalism
a way of organizing medieval society in which land was granted in return for loyalty and military service, and peasants worked the land for lords. used in: part 6.

g

genocide
acts committed with the intent to destroy, in whole or in part, a national, ethnic, racial, or religious group. the word was coined in 1944. used in: part 9, part 10.
genome
the complete set of dna in a living thing. see also: dna. used in: part 11.
glasnost
"openness": gorbachev's policy of allowing more free discussion in the soviet union. see also: perestroika. used in: part 11.
globalization
the growing connection of the world's economies, cultures, and peoples through trade, travel, and communication. used in: part 9, part 11.
great depression
the worldwide economic collapse that began with the 1929 wall street crash and lasted through most of the 1930s. used in: part 10.
great oxidation event
the rise of oxygen in earth's atmosphere about 2.4 billion years ago, caused by photosynthesizing microbes. used in: part 1.
guild
an association of craftworkers or merchants in a town that set rules, prices, and standards for its trade. used in: part 6.

h

hellenistic
describing the blend of greek and near eastern culture that spread after the conquests of alexander the great. used in: part 5.
hieroglyphs
the picture-based writing system of ancient egypt. see also: cuneiform. used in: part 4.
hijra
muhammad's move from mecca to medina in 622 ce, which marks the start of the islamic calendar. used in: part 6.
holocaust
the systematic murder of six million jews by nazi germany and its collaborators during the second world war. see also: genocide. used in: part 10.
holocene
the current geological epoch, which began when the last ice age ended about 11,700 years ago. used in: part 2, part 3.
hominin
any member of the human family tree after it split from the line leading to chimpanzees, including extinct species. see also: australopithecus. used in: part 2.
humanism
a renaissance movement that studied greek and roman writings and focused on human abilities and life in this world. used in: part 7.
hunter-gatherers
people who live by hunting wild animals and gathering wild plants instead of farming. used in: part 2, part 3.

i

ice age
a long period when the earth is cold enough for large ice sheets to cover parts of the continents. in everyday use, the last glacial period. used in: part 2.
imperialism
the policy of extending a country's power by taking over other lands, directly or through economic control. see also: colonialism. used in: part 9.
indulgence
in the medieval catholic church, a grant that reduced the punishment for sins, sometimes sold for money. see also: reformation. used in: part 7.
industrial revolution
the shift from hand production to machines, factories, and fossil-fuel power, which began in britain in the late 1700s. used in: part 8.
iron age
the period when iron became the main metal for tools and weapons, following the bronze age. see also: bronze age. used in: part 4.
irrigation
bringing water to fields through canals, ditches, or pipes. used in: part 3, part 4.

j

janissaries
the elite infantry of the ottoman empire, originally boys taken from christian families and trained as soldiers. used in: part 7.

l

liberalism
a political philosophy based on individual rights, elected government, the rule of law, and usually free markets. used in: part 9.

m

magna carta
the charter english barons forced king john to accept in 1215, which put the king under the law. used in: part 6.
mandate of heaven
the chinese idea that heaven grants the right to rule to a just ruler and takes it away from a bad one. used in: part 4.
mass production
making large numbers of identical products, often on an assembly line. used in: part 9.
mercantilism
the economic idea that a nation grows rich by exporting more than it imports and by using colonies as sources of raw materials. used in: part 8.
mesoamerica
the cultural region covering central and southern mexico and central america, home of the olmecs, maya, and aztecs. used in: part 4.
middle passage
the voyage across the atlantic that enslaved africans were forced to make, in brutal conditions. used in: part 7.
monotheism
belief in a single god. see also: polytheism. used in: part 4, part 6.
mummification
preserving a dead body by drying it, as the egyptians did so the dead could live on in the afterlife. used in: part 4.

n

nationalism
the belief that people who share a language, culture, or history should have their own state, and loyalty to that nation. used in: part 8, part 9.
natural selection
the process by which living things better suited to their environment tend to survive and have more offspring, so their traits spread. see also: evolution. used in: part 9.
neanderthals
a species of human that lived in europe and western asia until about 40,000 years ago. see also: hominin. used in: part 2.
neolithic
the "new stone age": the period of the first farming communities, still using stone tools. see also: agricultural revolution, paleolithic. used in: part 3.
nomads
people who move from place to place instead of settling, often with herds of animals.

o

oracle bones
animal bones and turtle shells used by shang kings to ask their ancestors questions; they carry the oldest chinese writing. used in: part 4.

p

paleolithic
the "old stone age": the long period of human history before farming, when people lived as hunter-gatherers. see also: neolithic.
pandemic
an outbreak of disease that spreads across many countries or the whole world. used in: part 10, part 11.
pangaea
a supercontinent that joined almost all of earth's land together, before breaking apart. used in: part 1.
papyrus
a writing material made in egypt from the stems of a water plant. used in: part 4.
partition
the division of a territory into separate states, especially the 1947 division of british india into india and pakistan. used in: part 11.
pax romana
the "roman peace": about two centuries of relative peace in the roman empire beginning with augustus. used in: part 5.
perestroika
"restructuring": gorbachev's program of reforms to the soviet economy and government. see also: glasnost. used in: part 11.
pharaoh
the title of the kings of ancient egypt, who were seen as gods on earth. used in: part 4.
photosynthesis
the process by which plants, algae, and some bacteria use sunlight to make food from water and carbon dioxide, releasing oxygen. used in: part 1.
plantation
a large farm growing one crop for sale, like sugar, tobacco, or cotton, often worked by enslaved or forced labor. used in: part 7.
polis
the greek word for a city-state. see also: city-state. used in: part 5.
polytheism
belief in many gods. see also: monotheism.
primary source
evidence from the time being studied, like a letter, a law, an inscription, or an object, as opposed to a later account of it.
primates
the group of mammals that includes lemurs, monkeys, apes, and humans. used in: part 1.
printing press
a machine for printing text from movable type, developed in europe by gutenberg around 1450; earlier printing existed in china and korea. used in: part 7.
propaganda
information, often biased or false, spread to win people over to a cause. used in: part 10.
protestant
a member of the western christian churches that broke away from the roman catholic church during the reformation. see also: reformation. used in: part 7.

r

radiocarbon dating
a method of dating once-living things up to about 50,000 years old by measuring how much radioactive carbon they still contain. used in: part 2, part 3, part 4.
reformation
the religious movement begun by martin luther in 1517 that split western christianity into catholic and protestant churches. see also: protestant, counter-reformation. used in: part 7.
renaissance
the "rebirth" of art and learning based on classical models, which began in italy and spread across europe. used in: part 6, part 7.
reparations
payments a defeated country is made to pay for damage caused in a war. used in: part 10.
republic
a state without a monarch, ruled by elected officials. used in: part 5, part 8.

s

samurai
the warrior class of pre-modern japan, who served lords called daimyo. used in: part 6.
satrapy
a province of the persian empire, run by a governor called a satrap. used in: part 5.
scientific revolution
the change in how nature was studied, through observation, experiment, and mathematics, associated with copernicus, galileo, and newton. used in: part 7.
segregation
the forced separation of people by race in schools, housing, transport, and public places. see also: apartheid. used in: part 9, part 11.
serf
a peasant bound to work a lord's land who could not leave it without permission. see also: feudalism. used in: part 6.
shogun
the military ruler of japan, who held the real power while the emperor was mostly a symbol. used in: part 6.
silk roads
the network of overland trade routes linking china, central asia, india, the middle east, and the mediterranean. used in: part 5, part 6.
slavery
a system in which people are owned as property and forced to work. see also: abolitionism, middle passage. used in: part 7.
socialism
a set of ideas holding that the community or state should own or control key parts of the economy and share wealth more equally. see also: communism, capitalism. used in: part 9.
steppe
the vast grasslands of central eurasia, home to horse-riding nomads. used in: part 6.
stromatolites
layered rock mounds built up by mats of microbes; some are among the oldest fossils on earth. used in: part 1.
suffrage
the right to vote. used in: part 8, part 9.
sultan
the title of a muslim ruler, used by the ottomans, the delhi sultanate, and others. used in: part 7.
supernova
the huge explosion of a dying star, which scatters heavy elements through space. used in: part 1.

t

terracotta army
thousands of life-sized clay soldiers buried with qin shi huang, the first emperor of china. used in: part 5.
totalitarianism
a system in which the state tries to control every part of life, public and private. see also: fascism. used in: part 10.
trench warfare
fighting from long lines of dug-in ditches, which led to stalemate on the western front in the first world war. used in: part 10.
tributary system
the way imperial china managed relations with neighbors, who sent gifts and acknowledged the emperor in return for trade and recognition. used in: part 7.

v

vedas
the oldest sacred texts of hinduism, composed in sanskrit and passed down orally for centuries. used in: part 4.

z

ziggurat
a huge stepped temple tower of ancient mesopotamia. used in: part 4.
