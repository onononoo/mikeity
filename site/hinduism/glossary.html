hinduism: glossary

words used in hinduism (18 terms). words linked in the story point here. the main glossary has the rest.

a · b · d · k · m · o · p · s · t · u · v · y

a

atman
the true self or soul, which is not born and does not die. see also: brahman.
avatar
a god coming down to earth in bodily form. vishnu has ten main avatars, including rama and krishna.

b

bhagavad gita
"the song of the lord": krishna's teaching to arjuna in the mahabharata, on duty, devotion, and the self.
bhakti
loving devotion to a personal god, such as vishnu, shiva, or the goddess.
brahman
the ultimate reality behind everything, of which all gods are forms. see also: atman.

d

dharma
the right order of things, and each person's duty within it.
diwali
the festival of lights in autumn, celebrating the victory of light over darkness and rama's return home.

k

kalpa
a day of brahma: 4.32 billion years, made of a thousand cycles of the four yugas. see also: yuga.
karma
the law that every action has consequences, in this life or the next. see also: samsara.

m

moksha
liberation from the cycle of rebirth, the highest goal of life. see also: samsara.

o

om
the sacred sound said at the start of prayers, seen as the sound of brahman.

p

puja
worship, at home or in a temple, with offerings of flowers, food, light, and incense.

s

samsara
the endless cycle of birth, death, and rebirth. see also: karma, moksha.

t

trimurti
the three great gods: brahma the creator, vishnu the preserver, and shiva the destroyer and renewer.

u

upanishads
philosophical texts at the end of the vedas, about the self and ultimate reality. see also: vedas.

v

vedas
the oldest scriptures, hymns and rituals in sanskrit, believed to have been heard by ancient seers. see also: upanishads.

y

yoga
"joining": paths of discipline of body and mind that lead toward union with the divine.
yuga
one of four ages in each cycle of time: satya, treta, dvapara, and kali, each shorter and darker than the last. see also: kalpa.
