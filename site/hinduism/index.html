hinduism

the family of traditions of india, called by many followers sanatana dharma, "the eternal way". it includes many gods seen as forms of one reality, and belief in karma, rebirth, and liberation.

no beginning and no end

in hindu tradition, the universe was not made once. it is created, kept, and dissolved again and again, in cycles without beginning or end. in one telling, vishnu sleeps on the great serpent shesha, floating on the cosmic ocean. a lotus grows from his navel, and on it brahma is born, who creates the worlds. in time shiva dissolves them, and the cycle begins again. these three gods are the trimurti.

the oldest scripture, the rigveda, has a more puzzled hymn of creation. it says that in the beginning there was "neither being nor non-being", and ends by asking who truly knows how it all began: "perhaps even the one who watches from the highest heaven does not know."

the ages of the world

time is measured on a vast scale. a cycle of four yugas, or ages, lasts 4,320,000 years:

agehow longwhat it is like
satya yuga1,728,000 yearsthe age of truth: dharma stands on four legs
treta yuga1,296,000 yearsdharma on three legs; the age of rama
dvapara yuga864,000 yearsdharma on two legs; the age of krishna
kali yuga432,000 yearsdharma on one leg; the age of strife, which is now

a thousand such cycles make one day of brahma, a kalpa of 4.32 billion years, and his night lasts as long. this is strikingly close to the scientific age of the earth, 4.54 billion years, though the tradition means something different: a rhythm, not a single beginning. by tradition, the kali yuga began in 3102 bce, when krishna left the world. at its end, kalki, the last avatar of vishnu, will come to begin a new age of truth.

the great stories

two epics carry much of hindu tradition. the ramayana, told by the sage valmiki, follows prince rama of ayodhya, an avatar of vishnu, whose wife sita is carried off by the demon king ravana. with the help of hanuman and an army of monkeys, rama rescues her. the mahabharata, said to be told by vyasa, is the story of a great war between two branches of one family. on the battlefield, the warrior arjuna loses heart, and his charioteer, krishna, teaches him the bhagavad gita, about doing one's duty without selfishness and about love for god.

what historians can show

hinduism has no single founder or starting date. its oldest texts, the vedas, were composed in early sanskrit from about 1500 bce and passed down by memory with extraordinary accuracy. they may draw on older traditions of the indus civilization and of india's many peoples. the upanishads, from about 800 bce, turned to the nature of the self, atman, and ultimate reality, brahman. the epics took their final form between about 400 bce and 400 ce, and the gupta age saw the first stone temples and the puranas, books of stories about the gods.

paths of devotion and knowledge

from about 600 ce, tamil poet-saints sang of bhakti, passionate personal devotion, and it spread across india. philosophers argued about how the self and god are related: adi shankara taught that they are one, and ramanuja that the soul is saved by loving god. later saints like chaitanya, mirabai, and tulsidas wrote and sang in the everyday languages of the people. hindu kings built temples as far away as cambodia, where angkor wat was dedicated to vishnu.

the modern age

under british rule, reformers like ram mohan roy worked to change practices they saw as wrong, and ramakrishna taught that all religions lead to god. his follower swami vivekananda brought hindu teaching to the west in 1893. mahatma gandhi drew on the gita in the struggle for independence. today about 1.2 billion people are hindu, and yoga, meditation, and ideas like karma are known around the world.

belief and practice

  • one ultimate reality, brahman, worshipped in many forms: vishnu, shiva, the goddess, and many more.
  • samsara, the cycle of rebirth, driven by karma, and moksha, liberation from it.
  • dharma: living rightly according to one's duties.
  • puja at home and in temples, the sacred sound om, pilgrimage, and festivals like diwali.
