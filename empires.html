part 9: industry and empire

the industrial revolution spread from britain to europe, the united states, and japan. machines, railways, steamships, and telegraphs made the world smaller and richer, but also created harsh working conditions. powerful industrial nations used their new strength to build huge empires, taking over nearly all of africa and much of asia.

the industrial world

industry spread from britain to belgium, france, germany, the united states, and later russia and japan. railways crossed continents. steamships crossed oceans quickly and reliably. the telegraph sent messages along wires in minutes, and cables were laid under the oceans to link continents.

later, a "second industrial revolution" brought steel, electricity, oil, and chemicals. new inventions included the telephone, the light bulb, the car, and the first airplanes. big businesses and huge factories appeared.

life in the industrial age

industrial cities grew fast and were often dirty, crowded, and unhealthy. factory workers, including young children, worked long hours for low pay in dangerous conditions. smoke filled the air and diseases like cholera spread through dirty water.

workers formed trade unions to fight for better pay, shorter hours, and safer workplaces. over time, governments passed laws limiting child labor, cities built sewers and clean water systems, and many countries began to offer free schooling. a growing middle class of managers, shopkeepers, and professionals also appeared.

people began to demand the right to vote. many men won it gradually, and the movement for women's suffrage grew, led by campaigners like emmeline pankhurst in britain and susan b. anthony in the united states. new zealand became the first country to let women vote in national elections.

new ideas

  • liberalism supported individual rights, elected governments, and free trade.
  • socialism argued that workers should share in the wealth they create and that the community should own key industries.
  • karl marx and friedrich engels wrote the communist manifesto. they said history was a struggle between classes and predicted that workers would overthrow the owners of factories and create a classless society. this idea, communism, would shape much of part 10 and part 11.
  • charles darwin explained evolution by natural selection in on the origin of species, showing how living things change over long periods of time. sadly, some people twisted his ideas into "social darwinism" to wrongly justify racism and empire.

nationalism and new nations

nationalism is the idea that people who share a language, culture, and history should have their own country. it became one of the most powerful forces of the age.

italy was united from many small states, led by figures like giuseppe garibaldi and his army of "redshirts". germany was united under prussia by its chancellor, otto von bismarck, through a series of short wars, and it quickly became europe's leading industrial and military power.

meanwhile, older empires like the ottoman empire, austria-hungary, and russia struggled to hold together many different peoples who wanted their own nations.

the united states grows

the united states expanded west across north america, buying land from france, taking land from mexico in a war, and pushing native american nations off their lands through war, broken treaties, and forced removal, such as the trail of tears.

the north and south split over slavery. when abraham lincoln was elected president, southern states broke away, and the american civil war began. it was the deadliest war in american history. lincoln's emancipation proclamation declared enslaved people in the rebel states free, the north won, and slavery was ended across the country. lincoln was assassinated soon after.

after the war, the united states became a giant industrial power, and millions of immigrants arrived from europe and asia. but formerly enslaved people and their children faced harsh segregation laws and violence, especially in the south.

the age of imperialism

industrial nations wanted raw materials like rubber, cotton, and metals, new markets for their goods, and national glory. many also wrongly believed they had a duty to "civilize" other peoples. armed with modern weapons like the machine gun, they built vast overseas empires.

the british empire became the largest empire in history, ruling about a quarter of the world's land and people. people said "the sun never sets on the british empire". france, the netherlands, belgium, germany, italy, portugal, spain, the united states, russia, and japan also took colonies.

the scramble for africa

at the start of this period, most of africa was still ruled by africans. then, at the berlin conference, european powers met, without inviting a single african, and drew lines on the map to divide the continent among themselves. within a short time, almost all of africa was under european rule. only ethiopia, which defeated an italian army at the battle of adwa, and liberia stayed independent.

colonial rule was often brutal. in the congo, which king leopold ii of belgium ran as his personal property, millions died from forced labor, violence, and hunger while producing rubber. across the continent, colonial borders cut through ethnic groups and kingdoms, causing problems that still last today.

china, japan, and india

china: britain sold opium in china to pay for tea. when china tried to stop it, britain attacked in the opium wars and won, forcing china to open its ports and hand over hong kong. western powers and japan carved china into "spheres of influence". the huge taiping rebellion killed millions. the qing dynasty grew weak, and it was finally overthrown, making china a republic and ending thousands of years of rule by emperors.

japan: american warships under commodore perry forced japan to open to trade. fearing it would be colonized like china, japan chose to modernize at incredible speed in the meiji restoration. it built factories, railways, and a modern army and navy. japan then defeated china and, shockingly to the west, russia, becoming an empire itself.

india: the british east india company took over most of india. after a large rebellion against it, which the british called the indian mutiny, the british government took direct control, and queen victoria became empress of india. this period of british rule is called the raj.

science and invention

  • louis pasteur and robert koch proved that germs cause disease, leading to vaccines and better hygiene.
  • anesthesia made surgery painless, and antiseptics made it much safer.
  • dmitri mendeleev created the periodic table of elements.
  • marie curie studied radioactivity and won two nobel prizes.
  • thomas edison developed the light bulb and the phonograph, and alexander graham bell the telephone.
  • the wright brothers made the first powered airplane flight.
  • albert einstein published his theory of relativity, changing our ideas of space and time.

by the end of this period, europe's empires ruled much of the world, and rivalries between the great powers were growing dangerous. the stage was set for part 10.
