before there were people, there was a very long story. the universe began, stars lit up, the earth formed out of dust and rock, and life slowly went from tiny single cells to fish, forests, dinosaurs, and mammals. this is the stage that all of human history is played on.

the big bang

the universe began with the big bang. everything that exists today, all the matter, energy, space, and time, was packed into an unimaginably hot and dense point, which then began to expand. it is still expanding now.

in the first moments, the universe was far too hot for atoms. as it cooled, the first simple particles formed, and then the first atoms: mostly hydrogen and helium, the two lightest elements. a faint glow left over from this early time, called the cosmic microwave background, can still be picked up by telescopes today.

stars and galaxies

gravity pulled clouds of hydrogen and helium together until they got so dense and hot that they began to burn through nuclear fusion. these were the first stars. stars gathered into huge groups called galaxies. our own galaxy is the milky way.

inside stars, lighter elements were fused into heavier ones like carbon, oxygen, and iron. when big stars died in giant explosions called supernovas, they scattered these elements across space. almost every atom in your body, apart from hydrogen, was made inside a star.

the birth of the earth

in one quiet corner of the milky way, a cloud of gas and dust collapsed and formed our sun. the leftover material spun around it in a flat disk, and bits of it clumped together to form the planets, including the earth.

the young earth was a violent place: molten rock, constant volcanoes, and huge impacts from space. one giant collision with a planet-sized object is thought to have thrown off the material that became the moon. over time the earth cooled, a solid crust formed, and water collected into the first oceans.

the first life

life began in the oceans. no one knows exactly how, but simple chemicals combined into molecules that could copy themselves, and eventually into the first living cells. these were tiny, simple microbes, like bacteria.

for a very long time, microbes were the only life on earth. some of them, called cyanobacteria, learned to use sunlight to make food, a process called photosynthesis. they released oxygen as waste. slowly, this oxygen filled the air and changed the planet forever, making complex life possible.

complex life

eventually, bigger and more complicated cells appeared, with a nucleus and other parts inside them. later still, cells began living together as one body. these were the first multicellular living things.

then came a sudden burst of new kinds of animals, known as the cambrian explosion. the seas filled with creatures that had shells, eyes, legs, and spines. many of the main body plans of animals alive today first appeared at this time, including the ancestors of all animals with backbones.

life on land

plants were the first to move onto land, followed by insects and other small creatures. later, some fish developed strong fins and lungs and crawled out of the water. they became the first amphibians.

huge swampy forests covered the land. when those plants died and were buried, they slowly turned into coal. from amphibians came reptiles, which laid eggs with hard shells and no longer needed to return to the water.

life was not always safe. several times, mass extinctions wiped out most living species. the worst one, called the great dying, killed off the large majority of life in the sea and on land.

the dinosaurs

after the great dying, reptiles called dinosaurs rose to rule the land. some were small and fast, others were the largest animals ever to walk the earth. flying reptiles called pterosaurs ruled the skies, and giant marine reptiles swam the seas. the first small mammals and the first flowering plants also appeared during this time. some dinosaurs evolved feathers, and one branch of them became the birds.

the age of dinosaurs ended when a giant asteroid slammed into the earth near what is now mexico. the impact threw dust and smoke into the sky, blocked the sun, and caused another mass extinction. all dinosaurs died out, except for the birds.

the age of mammals

with the dinosaurs gone, mammals spread out and grew into many new forms: whales, horses, cats, elephants, bats, and more. among them was a group of tree-living mammals called primates, with grasping hands and forward-facing eyes.

over a very long time, some primates became monkeys, and others became apes. one line of apes would lead to chimpanzees, and another, separate line would lead to us. that story continues in part 2.
