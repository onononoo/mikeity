the western roman empire fell, and europe broke into many small kingdoms. meanwhile, a new religion, islam, spread from arabia across three continents, china had a golden age, the mongols built the largest land empire in history, and rich kingdoms rose in africa and the americas. the period ended with a terrible plague.

the fall of rome

the roman empire became too big to rule easily, and it was split into a western half and an eastern half. the western half faced money troubles, civil wars, and pressure from germanic peoples like the goths, vandals, and franks, who were themselves being pushed by the huns from central asia.

rome itself was sacked, and eventually the last western emperor was removed from power. western europe split into many germanic kingdoms. cities shrank, trade fell, and much learning was lost in the west. the eastern half of the empire, however, carried on.

the byzantine empire

the eastern roman empire, known as the byzantine empire, ruled from the great city of constantinople (modern istanbul). it spoke greek and was deeply christian. the emperor justinian won back parts of the west for a while, wrote down roman law in one great collection, and built the huge church of the hagia sophia.

the byzantines protected ancient greek learning and spread orthodox christianity to eastern europe and russia. over time, the christian church split between the catholic west, led by the pope in rome, and the orthodox east. the empire lasted until constantinople fell to the ottoman turks, which happens in part 7.

the rise of islam

in the arabian peninsula, in the trading city of mecca, a merchant named muhammad said he received messages from god (allah) through the angel gabriel. these were collected into the quran, the holy book of islam. muhammad taught that there is only one god and that people should submit to his will. forced out of mecca, he and his followers moved to medina, then returned and took mecca.

after muhammad died, his successors, called caliphs, led arab armies out of arabia. in a short time they conquered persia, egypt, syria, north africa, and spain. disagreement over who should lead split muslims into two main branches, sunni and shia, a split that still exists.

under the abbasid caliphs, the city of baghdad became the center of an islamic golden age. in the "house of wisdom", scholars translated greek, persian, and indian books into arabic. muslim thinkers made big advances in algebra, astronomy, medicine, chemistry, and optics. the word "algebra" comes from arabic. muslim spain, called al-andalus, was also a great center of learning.

medieval europe

in western europe, the frankish king charlemagne united much of the region and was crowned emperor by the pope. after his empire broke up, vikings from scandinavia raided and traded across europe. they settled in england, ireland, france (normandy), and russia, and even reached iceland, greenland, and north america.

society was organized under feudalism. kings gave land to lords in return for military service. knights fought for lords. most people were peasants or serfs, who worked the land and could not leave it. the catholic church was very powerful, and monasteries kept learning alive by copying books by hand.

in england, the normans conquered the country and built castles everywhere. later, english barons forced king john to sign the magna carta, which said even the king had to follow the law. over time, trade and towns grew again, universities were founded, and huge gothic cathedrals were built.

the crusades

the byzantine emperor asked the pope for help against the seljuk turks. the pope called on christian knights to go and capture jerusalem, which was holy to christians, jews, and muslims. these holy wars were called the crusades.

the first crusade captured jerusalem with great bloodshed. later, the muslim leader saladin won it back. there were several more crusades, but in the end they failed. one crusade even attacked and looted christian constantinople. the crusades increased contact and trade between europe and the middle east, but also left a legacy of bitterness.

china and east asia

china was reunited under the short-lived sui dynasty, which built the grand canal. then came the tang dynasty, a golden age of poetry, art, and trade. its capital, chang'an, was one of the largest and most international cities in the world. buddhism flourished.

the song dynasty that followed was one of the most advanced societies on earth. the chinese invented or improved gunpowder, the compass, printing with movable type, and paper money. rice farming boomed and cities grew huge. government officials were picked through tough exams on confucian texts.

chinese culture spread to its neighbors. korea and vietnam borrowed chinese writing and ideas. in japan, a ruling class of warriors called samurai rose to power, led by military rulers called shoguns, while the emperor became mostly a symbol.

the mongols

on the grasslands of central asia, a leader named temüjin united the nomadic mongol tribes and took the title genghis khan. the mongols were brilliant horse archers. they conquered northern china, central asia, and persia with terrifying speed and brutality.

his sons and grandsons kept going, creating the largest land empire in history, stretching from korea to eastern europe. they destroyed baghdad and ended the abbasid caliphate. genghis's grandson kublai khan conquered all of china and started the yuan dynasty. his attempts to invade japan failed when storms, which the japanese called kamikaze or "divine winds", wrecked his fleets.

once the fighting stopped, the mongols kept the silk roads safe, and trade boomed. the italian merchant marco polo traveled to kublai khan's court, and his book amazed europeans. the mongol empire later broke into separate parts, such as the golden horde in russia.

africa

  • west africa: trade in gold and salt across the sahara, carried by camel caravans, made great empires rich. first came ghana, then mali. mali's ruler mansa musa is often called the richest person in history. when he made his pilgrimage to mecca, he gave away so much gold that its price in egypt dropped. the city of timbuktu became a great center of islamic learning. later came the songhai empire.
  • east africa: swahili city-states like kilwa and mombasa traded gold, ivory, and other goods across the indian ocean with arabia, persia, india, and china.
  • southern africa: the kingdom of great zimbabwe built huge stone walls without mortar and grew rich on gold and cattle.
  • ethiopia: christian kings built amazing churches carved straight down into solid rock at lalibela.

the americas

the classic maya cities declined, possibly because of drought, war, and overuse of land, but maya cities like chichen itza continued in the north of the yucatán.

in central mexico, the aztecs founded their capital, tenochtitlan, on an island in a lake. it became one of the largest cities in the world, with canals, causeways, floating gardens, and great temples. the aztecs ruled a large empire and made human sacrifices to their gods.

in south america, the inca built a vast empire along the andes mountains. they had no wheels for transport and no writing as we know it, but they built thousands of miles of roads, rope bridges, and mountain cities like machu picchu. they kept records with knotted strings called quipu.

in north america, the mississippian culture built the city of cahokia with giant earth mounds, while the ancestral puebloans built cliff dwellings in the southwest.

india and southeast asia

india was divided among many kingdoms. the chola dynasty in the south built a strong navy and great hindu temples. muslim turkish rulers invaded from the north and set up the delhi sultanate. in southeast asia, the khmer empire built angkor wat in cambodia, the largest religious monument in the world.

the black death

a deadly plague spread from central asia along the trade routes the mongols had opened. it reached the black sea, then italy, and swept across the middle east and europe. this was the black death. it probably killed around a third or more of europe's population, and huge numbers across asia and north africa.

whole villages were emptied. but with so many people dead, workers were in short supply, so surviving peasants could demand better pay and more freedom. feudalism began to weaken, and europe slowly moved toward the new age of part 7.
