# part 10. 1914 to 1945.

== the thirty-one years {#overview}

between 1914 and 1945, the world went through two world wars, a revolution that created the first communist state, a flu pandemic, a global economic collapse, the rise of fascist dictatorships, and the holocaust. tens of millions died. some historians see the whole period as a single "second thirty years' war", with the first world war's unfinished business leading directly to the second.

== the road to war {#causes}

historians often sum up the long-term causes of the first world war with four words: militarism, alliances, imperialism, and nationalism.

- **militarism**: the great powers built huge conscript armies and made detailed war plans. britain and germany raced to build battleships.
- **alliances**: europe was divided into two armed camps. france, russia, and britain formed the triple entente. germany, austria-hungary, and italy formed the triple alliance.
- **imperialism**: rivalry over colonies, especially between germany and britain and france, created crises and suspicion.
- **nationalism**: nations like the serbs wanted to unite their peoples in their own state, threatening the multinational austro-hungarian empire. great-power pride made compromise hard.

on 28 june 1914, archduke franz ferdinand, heir to the austro-hungarian throne, was shot dead in sarajevo by gavrilo princip, a young bosnian serb nationalist. austria-hungary, backed by germany, issued an ultimatum to serbia and then declared war. russia mobilized to support serbia. germany declared war on russia and france, and invaded belgium to reach france quickly. britain declared war on germany. within six weeks of the shooting, the great powers were at war.

== the first world war {#ww1}

=== the western front

everyone expected a short war, "over by christmas". germany's plan to knock out france quickly failed at the battle of the marne in september 1914. both sides then dug in. soon two lines of trenches stretched from the english channel to switzerland.

[[trench warfare]] was a stalemate. defenders with machine guns, barbed wire, and artillery could almost always stop attacks. soldiers lived in mud, among rats, lice, and corpses. attacks across "no man's land" cost enormous numbers of lives for tiny gains. at verdun in 1916, the germans tried to "bleed france white"; about 700,000 soldiers were killed or wounded over ten months. on the first day of the battle of the somme, 1 july 1916, the british army suffered about 57,000 casualties, nearly 20,000 of them killed.

new weapons made the war even deadlier: poison gas, flamethrowers, tanks, submarines, and airplanes, first for spotting and then for fighting and bombing.

=== a world war

the war was fought far beyond europe. on the eastern front, russia and germany fought a war of movement across huge distances. the ottoman empire joined germany; allied troops, including many from australia and new zealand, landed at gallipoli in 1915 and were forced to withdraw after heavy losses. britain encouraged an arab revolt against the ottomans. fighting spread across africa, the middle east, and the pacific. millions of soldiers and laborers came from the colonies: more than a million indians served in the british army.

during the war, the ottoman government carried out the armenian [[genocide]]. armenian men were massacred, and women, children, and the elderly were sent on death marches into the syrian desert. around a million armenians died.

=== the home front

this was "total war". whole economies were turned over to war production. governments controlled industries, rationed food, and used [[propaganda]] to keep up morale. with millions of men at the front, women worked in factories, including making shells, and on farms, which helped them win the vote in several countries after the war.

=== the end

in 1917, german submarines began sinking all ships near britain, including american ones, and germany secretly proposed an alliance with mexico. the united states declared war in april 1917. the same year, russia collapsed into revolution and left the war. in spring 1918, germany launched a last great offensive in the west, which failed. with fresh american troops arriving, its allies surrendering, and its people starving under a british naval blockade, germany signed an [[armistice]] on 11 november 1918.

about 9 million soldiers died, and millions of civilians. then, in 1918-1920, an influenza [[pandemic]] spread around the world, killing an estimated 50 million people, more than the war itself.

== the russian revolution {#russia}

russia entered the war badly unprepared. by early 1917, millions of soldiers had died, food was short, and faith in tsar nicholas ii had collapsed. in february 1917 (march by the western calendar), strikes and mutinies in petrograd forced the tsar to abdicate. a provisional government took over but kept fighting the war.

the [[bolsheviks]], a radical marxist party led by [[@vladimir lenin]], promised "peace, land, and bread". in october 1917 (november in the west), they seized power in petrograd. they made peace with germany, giving up huge territories, and took over land and industry.

a brutal civil war followed (1918-1921) between the bolshevik "reds" and a mix of opponents, the "whites", supported by foreign troops. the bolsheviks won, using terror against their enemies; the former tsar and his family were shot in 1918. in 1922, the union of soviet socialist republics, the soviet union, was founded: the world's first communist state.

after lenin died in 1924, [[@joseph stalin]] outmaneuvered his rivals and became dictator. from 1928, he forced peasants onto collective farms and pushed through breakneck industrialization with five-year plans. collectivization caused famines that killed millions, especially in ukraine, where it is known as the holodomor, and in kazakhstan. in the great terror of 1936-1938, about 750,000 people were shot, and millions were sent to the gulag, a vast system of prison labor camps. the soviet union became a [[totalitarianism|totalitarian]] state and an industrial power.

== a troubled peace {#peace}

in 1919, the victors met in paris. the treaty of versailles blamed germany for the war and forced it to give up land and all its colonies, disarm, and pay [[reparations]]. many germans considered it a humiliating, unjust "dictated peace".

four empires collapsed: the german, austro-hungarian, russian, and ottoman. new states appeared in central and eastern europe, like poland, czechoslovakia, and yugoslavia, based on the idea of national self-determination promoted by the american president woodrow wilson. but the principle was applied mostly in europe. the ottoman arab lands were divided between britain and france as "mandates", ignoring wartime promises of arab independence; britain had also promised, in the balfour declaration of 1917, to support a "national home for the jewish people" in palestine. colonial peoples who had hoped for self-rule were bitterly disappointed.

the league of nations was founded to settle disputes peacefully. but the united states never joined, and the league had no army of its own.

from the ruins of the ottoman empire, [[@mustafa kemal atatürk]] led a war of independence and founded the republic of turkey in 1923, which he made into a secular, modernizing state. in india, [[@mahatma gandhi]] led mass campaigns of non-violent resistance, satyagraha, against british rule, such as the salt march of 1930.

== the great depression {#depression}

the 1920s were a boom time in the united states, with cars, radios, jazz, and a soaring stock market. in october 1929, the wall street stock market crashed. banks failed, businesses went bankrupt, and trade collapsed as countries raised tariffs to protect their own industries. the [[great depression]] spread around the world. at its worst, a quarter of american workers were unemployed, and germany's unemployment was even higher. prices for farm products and raw materials collapsed, devastating colonies and latin america.

in the united states, [[@franklin d. roosevelt]], elected in 1932, launched the new deal: public works programs, banking reforms, and social security. in many other countries, desperate people turned to extreme parties that promised to smash the system.

== the rise of fascism {#dictators}

=== italy

[[fascism]] was born in italy. [[@benito mussolini]], a former socialist, founded a movement that glorified the nation, violence, and war, and demanded obedience to a single leader. his black-shirted squads beat and killed socialists. in 1922, after the fascists' "march on rome", the king made mussolini prime minister. he soon made himself dictator, "il duce". in 1935-1936 italy invaded ethiopia, using poison gas against soldiers and civilians. the league of nations did almost nothing.

=== germany

in germany, the weimar republic, the democracy created after the war, was weakened by the resentment over versailles, political violence, catastrophic inflation in 1923, and then the depression. [[@adolf hitler]], leader of the national socialist (nazi) party, blamed germany's problems on jews, communists, and the treaty of versailles. he promised to make germany great again and win "living space" in eastern europe.

the nazis became the largest party in parliament, and in january 1933, hitler was appointed chancellor by conservative politicians who thought they could control him. within months, he had used a fire at the parliament building as an excuse to suspend civil rights, banned other parties, and made himself dictator. the nazis built a [[totalitarianism|totalitarian]] police state, with the gestapo secret police and concentration camps for opponents. jews were stripped of their citizenship by the nuremberg laws of 1935, and in the kristallnacht pogrom of november 1938, synagogues and jewish shops were destroyed across germany, and about 30,000 jewish men were sent to camps.

=== japan and spain

in japan, military leaders gained increasing control of the government. in 1931, the army seized manchuria. in 1937, japan launched full-scale war on china. when the chinese capital nanjing fell, japanese troops massacred tens of thousands, perhaps hundreds of thousands, of civilians and prisoners, and raped tens of thousands of women.

in spain, a civil war (1936-1939) between the elected republican government and nationalist rebels led by general francisco franco became a rehearsal for the wider war. hitler and mussolini sent planes and troops to franco; the soviet union aided the republic. german bombers destroyed the basque town of guernica. franco won and ruled spain as a dictator until 1975.

=== appeasement

hitler broke the treaty of versailles by rebuilding the army and sending troops into the rhineland. in 1938, germany annexed austria. then hitler demanded part of czechoslovakia. at munich, britain and france agreed, hoping to satisfy him and avoid war. this policy of [[appeasement]] failed: in march 1939, hitler took the rest of czechoslovakia.

== the second world war {#ww2}

=== germany conquers europe

in august 1939, hitler and stalin shocked the world by signing a non-aggression pact, secretly agreeing to divide eastern europe between them. on 1 september 1939, germany invaded poland. britain and france declared war. the soviet union invaded poland from the east.

in the spring of 1940, germany used "blitzkrieg", rapid attacks by tanks and aircraft, to conquer denmark, norway, the netherlands, belgium, and france in a few weeks. british and french troops were evacuated from dunkirk. britain, now led by [[@winston churchill]], fought on alone. in the battle of britain in the summer of 1940, the royal air force defeated the german air force, and hitler postponed his invasion. german bombers then attacked british cities in the blitz.

=== the eastern front

in june 1941, hitler broke his pact with stalin and invaded the soviet union with more than three million troops. german forces reached the outskirts of moscow and besieged leningrad, where about a million civilians died of starvation. this was a war of annihilation: the nazis planned to kill, enslave, or starve tens of millions of slavs.

the turning point came at stalingrad in 1942-1943, where the soviets surrounded and destroyed an entire german army. from then on, the red army pushed west. the eastern front was the largest and deadliest land war in history; the soviet union lost about 27 million people.

=== the pacific war

on 7 december 1941, japan attacked the american fleet at pearl harbor in hawaii, and rapidly conquered the philippines, malaya, singapore, burma, and the dutch east indies. the united states entered the war against japan and its allies germany and italy. the war was now truly global, between the allies (above all the united states, the soviet union, britain, and china) and the axis (germany, italy, japan).

in june 1942, the american navy destroyed four japanese aircraft carriers at midway, turning the tide in the pacific. americans then fought island by island toward japan in brutal battles like guadalcanal, iwo jima, and okinawa. in occupied asia, japan committed many atrocities, including forced labor, biological warfare experiments, and forcing hundreds of thousands of women into sexual slavery as so-called "comfort women".

=== the allies advance

allied forces defeated the axis in north africa and invaded italy in 1943; mussolini was overthrown and later killed. on 6 june 1944, "d-day", the largest seaborne invasion in history landed about 156,000 allied troops on the beaches of normandy in france. paris was liberated in august. allied bombers devastated german cities, killing hundreds of thousands of civilians.

== the holocaust {#holocaust}

the [[holocaust]] was the systematic, state-organized murder of six million jews by nazi germany and its collaborators, about two thirds of the jews of europe.

it developed in stages. first came persecution: the stripping of rights, boycotts, and violence. after the invasion of poland, jews were forced into overcrowded, sealed-off ghettos, where many died of hunger and disease. after the invasion of the soviet union, mobile killing units, the einsatzgruppen, with local collaborators, shot more than a million jews beside pits and ravines; at babi yar, near kyiv, nearly 34,000 were murdered in two days. at the wannsee conference in january 1942, nazi officials coordinated the "final solution": the murder of all the jews of europe.

jews from across europe were deported in cattle cars to death camps in occupied poland, such as treblinka, sobibor, belzec, and auschwitz-birkenau, where most were murdered in gas chambers on arrival. at auschwitz alone, about 1.1 million people were killed, about a million of them jews. [[@anne frank]], a jewish girl who hid with her family in an amsterdam attic for two years, was betrayed, deported, and died in bergen-belsen in 1945; her diary became one of the most-read books in the world.

the nazis also murdered roma and sinti people, disabled people, soviet prisoners of war (about three million), polish and other slavic civilians, gay men, jehovah's witnesses, and political opponents.

there was resistance: the warsaw ghetto uprising in 1943, revolts at treblinka and sobibor, partisans in the forests, and people who risked their lives to hide jews. but most of the world did little. after the war, the word [[genocide]], coined in 1944 by the lawyer raphael lemkin, was made a crime under international law.

== the end of the war {#end}

=== germany

in early 1945, soviet forces liberated auschwitz and closed in on berlin from the east as the western allies crossed the rhine. on 30 april, hitler killed himself in his bunker. germany surrendered unconditionally on 8 may 1945.

=== the atomic bomb

scientists had discovered that splitting uranium atoms could release enormous energy. fearing that germany would build a bomb first, the united states ran a vast secret program, the manhattan project. on 16 july 1945, the first atomic bomb was tested in the new mexico desert. on 6 august, an american bomber dropped an atomic bomb on hiroshima, and on 9 august another on nagasaki. by the end of the year, the two bombs had killed about 110,000 to 210,000 people. the soviet union declared war on japan on 8 august. on 15 august, emperor hirohito announced japan's surrender, formally signed on 2 september 1945.

=== the cost

the second world war was the deadliest conflict in history. an estimated 70 to 85 million people died, about 3 percent of the world's population, most of them civilians. the soviet union, china, poland, and germany suffered the greatest losses. cities across europe and asia lay in ruins, and tens of millions of people were refugees.

at trials in nuremberg and tokyo, leaders of germany and japan were tried for war crimes and "crimes against humanity", a new idea in international law. the war left two superpowers, the united states and the soviet union, facing each other across a divided europe, and a new weapon that could destroy civilization. that is where [[part:the-modern-world|part 11]] begins.

== how we know {#evidence}

- **government archives**, including the nazis' own meticulous records, used as evidence at nuremberg.
- **testimony of survivors**, gathered in huge oral history projects, and diaries and letters written at the time.
- **photographs and film**, including footage shot by allied soldiers at the liberated camps.
- **the soviet archives**, opened in part after 1991, revealing the scale of stalin's crimes.
- **physical evidence**: the camps themselves, mass graves, and ruins.

== questions scholars still argue about {#debates}

- **who was to blame for the first world war?** germany, austria-hungary, russia, all the great powers, or a system that nobody controlled?
- **was the treaty of versailles too harsh, or not harsh enough?**
- **could appeasement have worked, or was war with hitler inevitable?**
- **was dropping the atomic bombs necessary to end the war?** this remains one of the most fiercely debated decisions in history.
- **how much did ordinary germans know about the holocaust?**
